Pop, measured

Five artists measured the same way: how long their biggest song lasted, where, whether their listening is one song or many, how many nights a city could fill and what a ticket cost in days of income.

Overwhelmed? Here's the short version
  • Every part answers the same questions from the same data, collected on one day, under one design written before the data were parsed.
  • The parts differ most in how spread their listening is, from six songs making half of one artist's streams to 57 for another.
  • Czech listeners keep English-language hits about as long as the world's middle and let hits in other languages pass quickly.
  • Nearly every show sold out for every artist, so the data cannot say where demand ends; tickets weighed most where incomes are lowest.

The parts

  1. Part 1 · Harry Styles · Spotify charts 2017–2026 · two tours, 2017–2023

    Harry Styles, measured: how long a hit lasts, where, and what a ticket costs

    As It Was spent 1 594 days in Spotify's global Top 200 and is still there; 5.5 % of number ones since 2017 lasted longer (95 % CI 1.4 % to 10.3 %). It outlasted the local median number one in 68 of 72 national charts; in Czechia it lasted 2.06 times as long, 35th of 72. Half of Harry Styles's streams come from 6 songs. 93 % of 165 Boxscore entries sold out, and the dearest ticket relative to income was in Philippines, 10.4 days of GDP per capita.

  2. Part 2 · Taylor Swift · Spotify charts 2017–2026 · two stadium tours · Europe's prices in 2024

    Taylor Swift, measured: a catalogue that sells out everything

    Cruel Summer spent 1 026 days in Spotify's global Top 200; 8.9 % of songs that also peaked at two since 2017 lasted longer (95 % CI 4.0 % to 14.5 %). It outlasted the local median number one in 37 of 68 national charts; in Czechia it lasted 1.03 times as long, 35th of 68. Half of Taylor Swift's streams come from 57 songs. 100 % of 85 Boxscore entries sold out, and the dearest ticket relative to income was in Japan, 1.3 days of GDP per capita.

  3. Part 3 · BTS · Spotify charts 2017–2026 · three tours, 2018–2026

    BTS, measured: a catalogue, not a hit

    Dynamite spent 780 days in Spotify's global Top 200; 23.8 % of number ones since 2017 lasted longer (95 % CI 17.5 % to 31.2 %). It outlasted the local median number one in 31 of 72 national charts; in Czechia it lasted 0.60 times as long, 60th of 72. Half of BTS's streams come from 41 songs. 93 % of 27 Boxscore entries sold out, and the dearest ticket relative to income was in Mexico, 7.3 days of GDP per capita.

  4. Part 4 · Bad Bunny · Spotify charts 2017–2026 · three tours, 2019–2026

    Bad Bunny, measured: a long hit, everywhere at once

    DtMF spent 634 days in Spotify's global Top 200 and is still there; 28.6 % of number ones since 2017 lasted longer (95 % CI 21.6 % to 36.1 %). It outlasted the local median number one in 34 of 65 national charts; in Czechia it lasted 0.24 times as long, 49th of 65. Half of Bad Bunny's streams come from 46 songs. 93 % of 87 Boxscore entries sold out, and the dearest ticket relative to income was in Honduras, 7.8 days of GDP per capita.

  5. Part 5 · Billie Eilish · Spotify charts 2017–2026 · three tours, 2019–2025

    Billie Eilish, measured: two long hits, five years apart

    BIRDS OF A FEATHER spent 867 days in Spotify's global Top 200 and is still there; 16.4 % of number ones since 2017 lasted longer (95 % CI 10.6 % to 22.9 %). It outlasted the local median number one in 56 of 71 national charts; in Czechia it lasted 1.69 times as long, 38th of 71. Half of Billie Eilish's streams come from 14 songs. 92 % of 118 Boxscore entries sold out, and the dearest ticket relative to income was in Philippines, 15.3 days of GDP per capita.

  6. Part 6 · all five artists · two exploratory models

    A hit lasts three times as long in its own language, and tickets barely follow income

    Across 348 artist–country pairs, with artist and country fixed effects, a song lasted 2.9 times as long in a country speaking its language (95 % CI 2.1–4.0). Across 428 tour entries, the average ticket rose with the host country's income at an elasticity of 0.22 (0.10 to 0.34), so a show cost several times more days of income in poorer countries. Exploratory: models fixed before estimation, inputs seen in parts 1–5.

Five maps

Each part's map shades the countries by how many times as long the artist's biggest song lasted in the national chart as the local median number one, blue where longer and grey where shorter, with circles for the average ticket in days of income. Side by side, they show where each artist's audience is: much of the world for Harry Styles and Billie Eilish, the English-speaking world and Asia more than Latin America for Taylor Swift, East and South-East Asia for BTS, and Latin America, Spain and the United States for Bad Bunny.

Three patterns stand out. The artists differ most in how their listening is spread: 6 songs make half of Harry Styles's streams and 57 of Taylor Swift's, with BTS and Bad Bunny close behind, fan bases that play albums and catalogues rather than singles. Czechia's place follows language: for the three English-language artists it sits near the middle of the ranking (35th of 72, 35th of 68, 38th of 71), for BTS and Bad Bunny well below it. And nearly every show sold out for every artist, so the series cannot say where demand ends; what it can say is that the same dollar price weighed several times more on fans in South-East Asia and Latin America than in Europe or North America.

Related

Does a concert move the charts?: a registered difference-in-differences that uses Spotify's daily charts to ask whether Harry Styles's first tour raised his share of each country's chart after he played there. The show week lifted it by about three quarters, and within three weeks the lift was gone. It is a separate study, not a part of the series.

How the series was registered

Part 1 was designed first, on 1 October 2026, before any chart day or ticket count was collected (cecad1c). The design for parts 2–5 and two questions added to all five followed the same evening, before any data were parsed (e890c27, 12417ae). The data were collected once, for all parts, and each part's results were computed from the same files with the same code. Part 2's study of prices during the Eras Tour has its own design, committed before any price index was downloaded (f5d702c). Changes after registration, including two data definitions added once results had been seen, are dated in the series design and in each part. Commit times are self-reported; there was no external review.

Data and code

Every part reads the same sources: kworb.net's totals of Spotify's global and national charts and its artist pages, the Wikipedia articles of the artists' tours with Billboard's Boxscore figures, and the World Bank's GDP per capita; part 2 adds Eurostat's harmonised price indices. The scripts in tools/pop/ collect, parse, analyse and draw every part, and each article's numbers are filled in from its published results file.
