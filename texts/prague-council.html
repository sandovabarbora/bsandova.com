Prague, measured · Part 1 of 5 · City Assembly roll calls, November 2022 – September 2026

Prague's council rarely says no

The city publishes how each of its 65 councillors voted on every resolution. Read member by member, four years of votes show a coalition that votes as one club, an opposition club that sides with it nearly nine times in ten, and an assembly that has almost stopped voting against anything. Dissent in Prague now mostly means not pressing the button.

Prague's City Assembly publishes its roll calls as open data: one row per vote, one column per councillor, and in each cell what that councillor did1. There are five possible entries. Hlas pro is a vote for, hlas proti against, zdržel se an abstention. Nehlasoval means the councillor was in the room and pressed nothing, and chyběl means absent. A resolution needs 33 votes for, a majority of all 65 seats, so the three ways of not voting for have the same legal effect: none of them counts. What differs is only what goes on the record.

This text reads the current term, from its first session in November 2022 to September 2026: 2 468 votes over 42 sessions. The file holds votes on substantive items only, not on the agenda or procedure, and in practice one final vote per resolution2. Rejected proposals and amendments are almost absent (two votes in the file failed), so this is a record of how the assembly reached the resolutions it passed, not of everything it argued about.

A coalition that votes as one club

Since 15 February 2023 Prague has been governed by SPOLU (ODS, TOP 09, KDU-ČSL), the Pirates and STAN, 36 of the 65 seats3. For each vote and each club, its position here is yes if more than half of its members present voted for, and anything else if not. Measured that way, STAN took the same side as SPOLU, the mayor's club, on 98 % of the votes, and the Pirates on 97 %.

The opposition is where it gets interesting. Praha sobě, with 11 seats, is not in the coalition and has not been this term, yet it took the same side as SPOLU on 88 % of the votes. ANO, the largest opposition club, did so on 64 %, and SPD on 52 %. The one councillor outside every club is Hana Kordová Marvanová, elected for SPOLU and expelled from its club in February 2023 after she did not vote for Bohuslav Svoboda as mayor4; she is counted on her own here, so her votes never move SPOLU's position.

Quarter by quarter, Praha sobě stays close to the coalition with two dips, in the third quarter of 2024 and the first of 2026. Both are sessions where a third of its members present pressed nothing and several were absent, not sessions where it voted against.

How Prague says no

Of all the entries recorded while councillors were present this term, 403 are "against". There are 1 269 abstentions and 25 734 cases of pressing nothing. Every club, in government or not, expresses disagreement almost entirely by not voting: for ANO, 98 % of its members' present non-yes votes are nehlasoval, for SPOLU 98 %, for the Pirates 84 %.

That was not always so. The same file exists for the three previous terms, and on the resolutions that passed, the rate of "against" has fallen in every term since 2010, from 1.06 per 100 councillors present to 0.28. In 2010–2014, 11.5 % of passed votes drew at least one vote against; in this term, 4.5 %. Not pressing anything rose over the same years from 12 to 18 per 100 present.

Two readings fit. One is that Prague's politics has become more consensual: most items are negotiated before they reach the floor, and what is left to vote on is agreed. The other is that disagreement has moved off the record. Pressing nothing is the one way of not supporting a resolution that nobody has to explain; a vote against is a position. The data cannot choose between them. What it can say is that a reader of the roll calls learns less about who opposed what than a reader in 2010 did.

What was checked

Clubs are the lists councillors were elected from5, checked against the clubs on the city's own list of councillors6; the only difference is Kordová Marvanová. A seat left vacant goes to the next substitute on the same list7, which is how the file's one unnamed column, voting from April 2025 after a STAN councillor left, is assigned to STAN. The city's list gives each councillor's current club only; a move between clubs earlier in the term that news reports missed would not show here. In 10 % of the rows, nearly all from 2026, the count of votes for printed in the file is one or two higher than the named columns add up to: replacements without a column. The quarter-by-quarter figure flags it.

Not checked: why abstentions fell after 2018. The dataset's contact at City Hall is the right person to ask. Every number here is produced by tools/zhmp/votes.py and tools/zhmp/terms.py from the published files.
