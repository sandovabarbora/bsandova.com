Delayed All the Time: Prague public transport as sound

A browser player that turns five days of Prague public transport in 2025 into synthesised techno, arranged by how far each moment runs from a normal day; the first, disco version remains selectable.

Overwhelmed? Here's the short version
  • The default engine compares every five-minute window with the median day of the same type, so the Meltdown plays 880 of its 1 440 minutes in the extreme state and Easter Monday 1 400 minutes sparse.
  • Its cut-offs were fixed a priori in standard deviations or round shares, and none was tuned by listening; other cut-offs would put the same days in other states. The track is an interpretation of the data, not a measurement.
  • Its noise is seeded, so a day at the same settings always gives the same track; the first version draws only its hat noise and reverb tail from random numbers. No audio is sampled.
  • The five dates are hard-coded in the extraction script; the computation that ranked them is not published. The piece makes no statistical claim.
  • Early arrivals count as negative delay in every mean, so they can move a day into a brighter key or a sparser arrangement.

The record was made alongside a master's thesis on delay persistence in the Prague network, which draws on the Golemio history of Prague Integrated Transport from 15 March to 8 September 2025: 121 197 794 records1, each a stop pass, one vehicle's segment from one stop to the next (checked in the data on 8 October 2026; the player's README calls them stop events). The record aggregates five days into 5-minute windows per mode: event count, mean arrival delay and its 90th percentile, which drives no sound. Arrivals delayed by less than −600 s or more than 3 600 s are left out, and early arrivals count as negative delay, so early running offsets lateness in every mean on this page. Only aggregates are published2.

The five days were chosen by rules written into the extraction script: the weekday closest to the median of both daily event count and daily mean delay (17 June), the days with the highest and lowest daily mean delay (30 April, Easter Monday 21 April), the first school day (1 September) and the holiday-season Sunday with the least traffic (13 July). The dates are fixed in the script, and neither the ranking computation nor the mean it ranks by is in the repository; its label for 30 April, "avg 158 s", matches the all-mode mean, while this page and the key use the bus, tram and trolleybus mean, 164 s. Whether the two extremes hold by that surface mean cannot be checked here, and the cause of the 30 April delays was not investigated2.

How the data maps to the sound

A day is 288 five-minute windows played as 288 beats, about two and a half minutes at the default 116 beats a minute, so a bar of four beats covers twenty minutes of data. The default engine, v2, treats each window as a distance from a normal day: the event-weighted mean delay of bus, tram and trolleybus against the median of the same window over all days of the same type (weekdays, Saturdays, or Sundays and holidays, the day itself included), in robust standard deviations, the median absolute deviation times 1.4826 and at least 5 s. The player averages this z score over three windows and sets every state once per bar, leaving a state only half a standard deviation below where it entered it2.

That z score sets the arrangement: below 0 the track is sparse, kick, bass and pad only; from 0 the hats, claps, stabs, plucks and lead join; from 1 it is lifted with an arpeggio and a second lead voice; from 2 it is extreme, entered through a two-bar breakdown. The groove follows the share of bus, tram and trolleybus stop events more than 90 s late: under 10 % the sixteenths run straight, from 10 % they swing, and above 25 % the beat drops to half-time, the two round shares standing for occasional and for systemic lateness.

Harmony and timbre climb a ladder on the same z, at most one rung a bar: the tonic below 0.5, the subdominant from 0.5, the dominant from 1, the dominant with added tensions from 2 and secondary dominants from 3, with a cadence on the way back down. Each rung opens the low-pass on the music, from 1.4 to 8 kHz, and adds saturation. The echo depends only on the hour: its feedback is 0.55 times the squared persistence, the correlation between a vehicle's delay at one stop and at the next, measured per hour over all 173 days, so it runs between 0.25 and 0.51 on every day alike. The melody rises one scale step above D4 for every 30 s of smoothed surface delay, stepwise through the current chord, ending phrases on the root or fifth. None of these cut-offs was tuned by listening2.

The first version, still selectable, plays the same days as disco: each mode is an instrument, bus and tram delay detune the chords and plucks, and a chorus starts wherever the smoothed surface delay passes a hand-set 68 % of the day's own peak (fig. 1). Its melody and loudness are scaled to each day's peak, so it compares moments within a day, where v2 compares each day with its normal counterpart.

The five days, their keys and states

Both engines play each day in D, in a mode chosen by its mean surface delay from the brightest to the darkest: under 40 s lydian, under 70 s ionian, under 100 s dorian, under 130 s aeolian, and phrygian above that, cut-offs also set by hand. Fig. 1 is recomputed from the aggregates embedded in the player, and its daily means match the table2.

The Meltdown's mean comes from one afternoon. Between 15:00 and 19:00 its surface delay averaged 304 s; without those four hours the day averages 114 s, which would put it in D aeolian, not phrygian. In the first version its only chorus is 15:20–18:25 and its morning rush plays as verse. Measured against a normal weekday, v2 lifts its morning at 08:40 and keeps it extreme from 09:20 to midnight, with the running z peaking at 9.68 at 18:3023.

The typical Tuesday spends 1 000 of its 1 440 minutes in the full state and never turns extreme. Back to School is extreme for 200 minutes, mostly between 09:40 and 12:40, and sparse through most of the afternoon. The holidays, compared with Sundays and holidays, are lifted or extreme only between 03:40 and 04:40, when each window holds a few hundred surface stop events. Other cut-offs would put the same days in other states; the track is a reading of the data, not a measurement3.
