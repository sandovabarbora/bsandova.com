Delayed All the Time: Prague public transport as sound

A browser player that turns five days of Prague public transport in 2025 into synthesised disco tracks, with each transport mode as an instrument and each day's key set by its mean surface delay.

Overwhelmed? Here's the short version
  • The same day always produces the same track: the mapping from data to sound contains no randomness, and no audio is sampled.
  • The rush-hour choruses and the keys come from thresholds set by hand, so the fit between the music and the working day reflects those choices and is not a finding about the network.
  • The piece makes no statistical claim. It shares its data source with the thesis on delay persistence but plays city-wide five-minute averages, which is a different object from the route-level trip dynamics the thesis estimates.
  • The mean delay behind each key counts early running as negative, so a day with many early departures sounds brighter than its lateness alone would make it.

The record was made alongside a master's thesis on delay persistence in the Prague network, which uses the same Golemio history of 121 197 794 vehicle-location records from 15 March to 8 September 20251. The record uses five days of it, counted as stop events, one per vehicle arrival at a stop, and aggregated into 5-minute windows per mode: the number of events, the mean arrival delay and its 90th percentile. Arrivals with a delay outside −600 to 3 600 s are left out before counting and averaging, and early arrivals enter the mean as negative values, so early running offsets lateness in every mean delay on this page. Only these aggregates are published; the raw records are not redistributed2.

The five days were chosen by rules written into the extraction script. The typical Tuesday, 17 June, is the weekday closest to the median of both the daily event count and the daily mean delay across the whole period; the Meltdown, 30 April, is the day with the highest mean delay in the data; Easter Monday, 21 April, the day with the lowest; Back to School, 1 September, the first school day of the year; and the Summer Sunday, 13 July, the holiday-season Sunday with the least traffic. The dates are fixed in the script, and the computation that ranked the days is not in the repository. The cause of the high delays on 30 April was not investigated2.

How the data maps to the sound

One day is 288 five-minute windows played as 288 beats, and at 116 BPM twenty-four hours take about two and a half minutes. Each mode is an instrument whose volume follows its number of stop events in the window, and some instruments also follow delay. The metro averages a delay of 7 s on a typical weekday and carries the kick drum, so the beat stops when the metro closes at night. Buses, with about 557 000 stop events on a weekday, play the chord stabs, whose detune in cents grows with the current bus delay. Trains average 111 s late on a typical weekday and play no instrument, though they remain in the player's own traffic chart2.

lead melodycity-wide event-weighted surface delay, quantised to the day's scale; the later the network runs, the higher the line
trainsno instrument; shown in the player's traffic chart only

The song structure follows one rule. When the smoothed, event-weighted mean delay of bus, tram and trolleybus exceeds 68 % of that day's maximum, the track moves into the chorus, and the two beats before each chorus break down into a snare roll. On the typical Tuesday the rule marks roughly 07:25–10:00 and 14:45–17:40, with the delay peaking at 145 s around 07:55. The 68 % threshold is a hand-set parameter, and the repository does not record how it was chosen, so its match with the two rush hours is a property of that choice. The arrangement, the swing and the mix are documented in the repository's README2.

The five days and their keys

Each day is played in D, in a mode chosen by its mean surface delay and running from bright to dark: under 40 s lydian, under 70 s ionian, under 100 s dorian, under 130 s aeolian, and phrygian above that. These cut-offs, too, were set by hand. Fig. 1 is recomputed for this page from the aggregates embedded in the player, and its daily means match the table2.
