The living call sheet

I built a whole film-production platform, script to schedule to crew. Then I cut it down to the one document that actually decides whether a shoot day works: the call sheet, issued the night before, revised at five in the morning, opened in a parking structure with no signal.

Why a call sheet

Film call sheets follow conventions that are decades old and load-bearing. Same-day revisions are the norm, named by colour or letter, so the sheet has to be a container of ordered revisions, not one mutable row, or "what changed since the version I printed" cannot be answered. Page counts are in eighths of a page, because scripts are timed that way, so they are stored as an integer eighth-count rather than a float an assistant director does arithmetic on by hand. Cast status codes (W, SW, WF, H, SWF, R) are a constrained enum. And offline is not a nice-to-have: call times are 5–6 am, locations are outside coverage, and this is the one document all 50 to 200 people need before they leave home1.

The first build

End-to-end production management for mid-budget films: script import, an AI-drafted breakdown, a stripboard schedule with a cascade engine, and the published call sheet in every crew member's pocket. Around it, the track a studio's procurement asks for: SSO/SAML with organisation domain mapping and post-login auto-provisioning, legal pages and a DPA, an in-app help hub, Sentry with a PII-scrubbing hook, opt-in PostHog analytics, and Stripe, Twilio, Resend, Frame.io and Google Calendar integrations. Real-time over WebSockets with unified subprotocol auth; an offline-first PWA for on-set conditions2.

An independent security review found two things worth keeping as lessons: an IS_DEV blocklist that failed open on a blank environment variable, inverted into an explicit allowlist; and SSO auto-provisioning gated on a client assertion, moved to a server-side SAML claim2.

The second build: one thing, done well

The skeleton keeps only the call-sheet slice: creating, revising and distributing shoot-day sheets, viewable offline. No budgeting, no breakdowns, no scheduling optimiser, no billing; what was deferred and why is written down before the code1. FastAPI, SQLAlchemy 2.0, Alembic and PostgreSQL behind; a React + Vite + TypeScript PWA in front, IndexedDB cache and a Workbox service worker, so a published day still renders in airplane mode. Sign-in by e-mail code, no password; owners invite editors and viewers and the API enforces the role. The API runs on Fly.io in Frankfurt with Supabase as plain Postgres; five architecture decision records cover the frontend stack, hosting, the editor, auth and roles3.

The company itself runs as one founder and an AI team, with an operating model, an approval contract and a rule that nothing with real-world effect happens without an approved item in an inbox file. That is written down too, in the repository, because a process that lives only in someone's head is not a process.

Try it

The live demo serves the PWA from GitHub Pages with a bundled sample shoot day. Open it on a phone, add it to the home screen, switch to airplane mode: the call sheet still renders from the cache. The AD editor is the other side of it.
