How to catch a fabricated quote

The wiki an agent keeps quotes its sources, hundreds of times. One day a quotation reached it that the source never contained, shaped exactly like the finding the brief was hoping for. The checker written the next day then nearly deleted three genuine quotations as fabrications. Both failures are in the tool now, as tests, and the tool is on PyPI.

Overwhelmed? Here's the short version
  • quotecheck is a Python tool on PyPI that checks whether a quotation is in its source. It was written after a fabricated ISACA quotation, produced by a PDF extraction that welded two columns together, reached the wiki; all 292 quoted passages across the fifteen CISA pages were then machine-checked.
  • It folds case, ligatures, hyphens, whitespace, accents and quotation marks, which covers the six classes of false alarm that once called genuine quotations fabricated. It answers FOUND, FOUND*, DRIFT or ABSENT, prints context in the source's own words, and counts drift as not found.
  • Lenient as it is, it still says ABSENT to the most-quoted sentence attributed to Viktor Frankl, "Between stimulus and response there is a space", which is not in Man's Search for Meaning. Its 20 tests cover each false-alarm class plus the cases that must fail.
  • Checking each quote before writing it does not catch splices of two real fragments. When two agents verified their own finished pages they found sixteen defects, fourteen of them splices, so batch mode checks every quoted span on the finished page.
  • It needs the source as text and is only as good as the extraction: a two-column scan still produces sentences that never existed, and the tool will find them. It cannot find a quotation in a book it has not been shown, and the 80 % drift threshold is a choice.

A fabricated ISACA quotation in the wiki

An agent reading the ISACA CISA Review Manual for the wiki produced, as ISACA's own words, the sentence "a security audit does not typically test information security in an organization but just indicates the extent of compliance with standards", which the manual does not contain. The clause "indicates the extent of compliance with standards" occurs once, in a different column of a two-column page. The manual is a 304 MB scanned PDF, and pdftotext -layout welds the two columns together line by line, producing sentences that read perfectly and never existed, and the reading agent accepted one of them1.

It survived the checks that existed, because it showed the compliance profession indicting itself in its own credentialing text, which was precisely the finding the brief had asked for. A fabrication tailored to a brief always looks like the best result in the report, so the strength of a claim is the wrong trigger for verification, and every quotation has to be checked. All 292 quoted passages across the fifteen CISA pages were then machine-checked. The quote was removed, the removal was recorded on the page, and every CISA page now carries a banner saying that its terminology and structure are reliable and its verbatim sentences are not1.

Six classes of false alarm

The first checker was grep, and the next day it reported three fabrications, all three of which were real quotations. "Refresh belies that analogy" begins a sentence, and the check was case-sensitive. "reduced vibration and good cooling" is hyphenated across a PDF line break and was extracted as reduced vibra- tion. The third spanned a line break, where the source has a newline and the typed phrase a space. False alarms are the more dangerous direction, because the purpose of checking a quote is to remove fabrications, and a checker that sees fabrication where there is none removes accurate material and records a correction that is itself wrong1.

Three more classes followed over the next days, each from a real miss. The first was ligatures, since the PDF stores the ﬀ of tradeoﬀ as one glyph. The second was accents, with Niccolo typed for the source's Niccolò. The third was quotation marks: Steinbeck's "For if 'Thou mayest'—it is also true that 'Thou mayest not'" had been transcribed without the inner marks, and straight, curly, single and double marks all varied between transcription and source. One further miss was not a folding rule at all. Hardy's "If a man has any genuine talent he should be ready to make almost any sacrifice" is in the book, but the scan reads talent he 4 should be ready, with a page number dropped into the sentence1.

Folding, the four answers and batch mode

The tool folds both the source and the phrase to a comparison key: lower case, ligatures expanded, hyphens and dashes dropped on both sides (the only symmetric fix for cross-\nvalidation), whitespace dropped, accents stripped, quotation marks removed. The fold keeps a map from every folded character back to its offset in the original, so the match is found on the key and the context is printed from the source's own text. Nothing normalised is ever shown, and that is what keeps a forgiving search honest, because a real sentence relayed with subtle rewording stays visible as reworded2.

The tool gives one of four answers, listed here in order of strictness. FOUND comes with the count and the surrounding sentence. FOUND* means the phrase was found in two adjacent halves with at most twelve folded characters between them, which is what page furniture from a scan looks like. Digits are deliberately not folded away, because 1910 is content in Stoner and folding them would let a fabrication through, and a splice of two real fragments from different parts of the book does not pass, because the halves are not adjacent. DRIFT comes with a similarity score and means that the phrase does not occur but a window of the source is at least 80 % similar; the tool then prints what the source actually says, and drift counts as not found. The last answer is ABSENT. In batch mode the tool pulls every blockquote and every quoted run of six or more words out of a Markdown note and checks them all, which is how a finished page is verified, as opposed to a single quote before it is written2.

$ quotecheck East-of-Eden/source.txt "For if Thou mayest—it is also true that Thou mayest not"
FOUND   1x  For if Thou mayest—it is also true that Thou mayest not
        …That throws it right back on a man. For if ‘Thou mayest’—it is also true that ‘Thou mayest not.’ Don’t you see?…

$ quotecheck mathsapology-hardy/source.txt "If a man has any genuine talent he should be ready to make almost any sacrifice"
FOUND*      If a man has any genuine talent he should be ready to make almost any …
        …If a man has any genuine talent he 4 should be ready to make almost any sacrifice in order to cultivate it to the full…

$ quotecheck CISA-Review-Manual/source.txt "a security audit does not typically test information security in an organization but just indicates the extent of compliance with standards"
ABSENT  a security audit does not typically test information security in an or…

$ quotecheck Mans-Search-for-Meaning/source.txt "Between stimulus and response there is a space"
ABSENT  Between stimulus and response there is a space

The four runs above are real, made against the extracted text of the four books3. The last one is a finding of its own, since the most-quoted sentence attributed to Viktor Frankl, "Between stimulus and response there is a space. In that space is our power to choose our response", is not in Man's Search for Meaning. It is widely reported as a misattribution, and the tool, although made lenient enough to find Hardy through a page number, still says ABSENT to it, which is the test a lenient checker has to pass before its FOUND can be trusted1, 4.

Splices, and verifying the finished page

Checking each quote before writing it did not prevent the ISACA fabrication, because both halves of a splice are genuinely in the source. When two agents were asked to verify their own finished pages, they found sixteen defects between them, fourteen of which were splices, two real fragments joined into a sentence the book never contains. Only re-extracting every quoted span from the finished page and checking it in place finds those. Batch mode exists for that, and verifying the page rather than the quote is now a standing rule of the vault1. The six classes of false alarm turned out to be one bug: the tool had been comparing transcriptions rather than words.

What it does not do

It needs the source as text, and it is only as good as the extraction; a two-column scan will still produce sentences that read well and never existed, and the tool will find them, because they are in the text it was given. The 80 % drift threshold is a choice that could have been set elsewhere. The tool does not judge whether a paraphrase was legitimate, only whether quotation marks were earned, and it cannot find a quotation in a book it has not been shown, so a quotation from any other source still has to be checked by a reader.
