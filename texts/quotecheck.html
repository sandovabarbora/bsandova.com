How to catch a fabricated quote

The wiki an agent keeps quotes its sources, hundreds of times. One day a quotation reached it that the source never contained, shaped exactly like the finding the brief was hoping for. The checker written the next day then nearly deleted three genuine quotations as fabrications. Both failures are in the tool now, as tests, and the tool is on PyPI.

The one that got in

An agent reading the ISACA CISA Review Manual for the wiki produced, as ISACA's own words, the sentence "a security audit does not typically test information security in an organization but just indicates the extent of compliance with standards." It is not in the manual. The clause "indicates the extent of compliance with standards" exists, once, in a different column of a two-column page; the manual is a 304 MB scanned PDF, and pdftotext -layout welds the two columns together line by line, manufacturing sentences that read perfectly and never existed. The reader accepted one1.

Why it survived the checks that existed: it was the compliance profession indicting itself in its own credentialing text, which is precisely the finding the brief had asked for. A fabrication tailored to a brief always looks like the best result in the report, so "this is a strong claim" is the wrong trigger for verification; every quotation is the right one. All 292 quoted passages across the fifteen CISA pages were then machine-checked; the quote was removed, the removal recorded on the page, and every CISA page now carries a banner saying that its terminology and structure are reliable and its verbatim sentences are not1.

The three that nearly went out

The first checker was grep. The next day it reported three fabrications, and all three were real. "Refresh belies that analogy" begins a sentence and the check was case-sensitive. "reduced vibration and good cooling" is hyphenated across a PDF line break, extracted as reduced vibra- tion. A quote spanning a line break, where the newline is not the space the checker typed. A false alarm is the dangerous direction: the purpose of checking a quote is to remove fabrications, so a checker that sees fabrication where there is none removes accurate material and records a correction that is itself wrong1.

Three more classes followed over the next days, each from a real miss. Ligatures: the PDF stores tradeoﬀ as one glyph. Accents: Niccolo typed for the source's Niccolò. Quotation marks: Steinbeck's "For if 'Thou mayest'—it is also true that 'Thou mayest not'" transcribed without the inner marks, and straight, curly, single and double all varying between transcription and source. And one that is not a folding rule at all: Hardy's "If a man has any genuine talent he should be ready to make almost any sacrifice" is in the book, and the scan reads talent he 4 should be ready, a page number dropped into the sentence1.

What the tool does

Fold, but show the original. Both the source and the phrase are folded to a comparison key: lower case, ligatures expanded, hyphens and dashes dropped on both sides (the only symmetric fix for cross-\nvalidation), whitespace dropped, accents stripped, quotation marks removed. The fold keeps a map from every folded character back to its offset in the original, so the match is found on the key and the context is printed from the source's own text. Nothing normalised is ever shown, which is what keeps a forgiving search honest: a real sentence relayed subtly reworded stays visible as reworded2.

Four answers, in order of strictness. FOUND, with the count and the surrounding sentence. FOUND*, the phrase in two adjacent halves with at most twelve folded characters between them: page furniture from a scan. Digits are deliberately not folded away, because 1910 is content in Stoner and folding them would let a fabrication through; and a splice of two real fragments from different parts of the book does not pass, because the halves are not adjacent. DRIFT, with a similarity score: the phrase does not occur, but a window of the source is at least 80 % similar, and the tool prints what the source actually says. Drift counts as not found. ABSENT. Batch mode pulls every blockquote and every quoted run of six or more words out of a Markdown note and checks them all, which is how a finished page is verified rather than a quote before it is written2.

$ quotecheck East-of-Eden/source.txt "For if Thou mayest—it is also true that Thou mayest not"
FOUND   1x  For if Thou mayest—it is also true that Thou mayest not
        …That throws it right back on a man. For if ‘Thou mayest’—it is also true that ‘Thou mayest not.’ Don’t you see?…

$ quotecheck mathsapology-hardy/source.txt "If a man has any genuine talent he should be ready to make almost any sacrifice"
FOUND*      If a man has any genuine talent he should be ready to make almost any …
        …If a man has any genuine talent he 4 should be ready to make almost any sacrifice in order to cultivate it to the full…

$ quotecheck CISA-Review-Manual/source.txt "a security audit does not typically test information security in an organization but just indicates the extent of compliance with standards"
ABSENT  a security audit does not typically test information security in an or…

$ quotecheck Mans-Search-for-Meaning/source.txt "Between stimulus and response there is a space"
ABSENT  Between stimulus and response there is a space

The four runs above are real, against the extracted text of the four books3. The last one is a finding in its own right: the most-quoted sentence attributed to Viktor Frankl, "Between stimulus and response there is a space. In that space is our power to choose our response", is not in Man's Search for Meaning. It is widely reported as a misattribution, and the tool, having been made lenient enough to find Hardy through a page number, still says ABSENT to it, which is the only test that counts1, 4.

The rule that followed

Checking each quote before writing it did not prevent the ISACA fabrication, and the reason is structural: both halves of a splice are genuinely in the source. When two agents were asked to verify their own finished pages, they found sixteen defects between them, fourteen of which were splices, two real fragments joined into a sentence the book never contains. Only re-extracting every quoted span from the finished page and checking it in place finds those, which is what batch mode is for, and it is now a standing rule of the vault: verify the page, not the quote1. Six classes of false alarm turned out to be one bug: the tool was comparing transcriptions rather than words.

What it does not do

It needs the source as text, and it is only as good as the extraction; a two-column scan will still produce sentences that read well and never existed, and the tool will find them, because they are in the text it was given. The 80 % drift threshold is a choice, not a law. It does not judge whether a paraphrase was legitimate, only whether quotation marks were earned. And it cannot find a quotation in a book it has not been shown, which is where a citation checker stops and a reader starts.
