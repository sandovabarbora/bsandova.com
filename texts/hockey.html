What the atlas does and comparable work

The atlas asks how deep the Czech professional hockey pool is against nine peer nations, when its NHL presence declined, where by position and age it is thin, whether young players get ice time at home, how Czech players fare abroad and which leagues the national team is drawn from. It is published at hockey.bsandova.com1. Hockey-Reference lists NHL players by birthplace2, and Elite Prospects keeps career records across leagues3; neither counts a nation per head against named peers across several leagues with one rule for nationality. The atlas uses the design of the football atlas4: the same ladder of leagues, per-head counts, a break model for the long series and a committed snapshot. It is exploratory and was not pre-registered.

Method

Sources. One fetcher per source: the NHL stats REST API5, the Liiga6 and SHL7 JSON statistics, hokej.cz for the Extraliga8, the DEL9 and National League10 statistics, Eurostat population on 1 January11 and the English Wikipedia roster articles for every World Championship and Olympics since 201012. Each source was fetched once, politely, into a raw cache; regular seasons only.

Ladder. Rung 1 is the NHL. Rung 2 is the SHL, Liiga, National League and DEL; "NHL + rung 2" is the five-league headline. The Czech Extraliga is the home league. A player counts in a season when he reaches at least 20 of 82 games, pro-rated to the league-season's schedule, the 95th percentile of skater games: 20 in a full NHL season, 12 in 2012/13, 17 in 2019/20 and 14 in 2020/21.

Nationality. One definition for every source, in this order: citizenship where a league publishes it (the NHL's nationalityCode, the Liiga's and SHL's nationality); a World Championship or Olympic roster since 2010; otherwise eligibility or licence (the Extraliga's foreigner flag, the DEL's licence from 2022/23, the National League's Swiss licence). Czechoslovak-era births take the state the NHL recodes them to.

Exclusions. The KHL is left out by design, which removes a real destination, most of all before 2014. The AHL is not fetched because its terms forbid automated access, and the Slovak Extraliga because its robots.txt blocks it; Slovakia is measured through its players in the covered leagues. Elite Prospects is not scraped, by its terms.

Linking. Records are joined across leagues on the name, with diacritics folded, plus the full birth date; then on first initial, surname and birth date; records without a birth date join on the name alone when exactly one person of that name played within four seasons. The build holds 17 991 people, 14 309 with a nationality; 189 carry conflicting codes, and in 50 of them an Extraliga "Czech" flag is overridden by citizenship or a roster.

The NHL series 1995/96–2025/26 is fitted with a Poisson local-level model with two step changes whose seasons are marginalised, the football atlas's series model, in PyMC13.

Worked example: Czechia in 2025/26

Twenty Czech players reached the games threshold in the NHL, 1.83 per million inhabitants, 4th of 10 and above the peer median of 1.21. Sweden (7.26) and Finland (7.25) have about four times the Czech rate. Counting the five leagues, Czechia has 53 players, 4.86 per million, 5th of 10 against a peer median of 3.551.

The Czech NHL group rose from 25 in 1995/96 to 58 in 2001/02 and has fallen to 20. The model dates a rise to 1998/99 (probability 0.39, level ×1.47, 90 % HDI 0.73–1.97; the HDI, highest-density interval, is the narrowest range that holds 90 % of the model's probability). The most probable season of the fall is 2009/10, with probability 0.09; 87 % of the fall's probability is spread over 2008/09–2023/24, and the probability that the step lowers the level is 0.80. The fitted level is 23.1, 43 % of its 2002/03 peak of 53.1. Finland and Sweden show only rises over the same seasons1.

No Czech player aged 21 or under reached the threshold in the NHL or rung 2 in 2025/26. The largest shortfall against the peer median is forwards aged 21 or under, 6.1 players, then forwards aged 22–25 (2.8) and defencemen aged 21 or under (2.0). Forwards aged 26–29 are furthest above the median: 15 players, 1.37 per million against 0.66, then defencemen aged 30 or over, 7 players against a median of 0.

Under-21 here means aged 20 or less. The National League and the DEL publish no birth dates, so their shares are not measured. Abroad, over 2020/21–2025/26, Czech NHL skaters played 0.98 times the median ice time of their league, season and position and scored 0.87 times its points per game; in the Liiga the ratios are 1.09 and 1.40, in the SHL 1.08 and 1.301.

Across 21 World Championship and Olympic rosters since 2010, Czech roster spots came on average 30 % from the NHL, 28 % from the Extraliga, 25 % from the KHL, 13 % from rung 2 and 3 % from other leagues; the Czech NHL share is 2nd of the ten nations. No KHL player has been on the roster since the 2022 World Championship, and the 2026 World Championship roster had 4 NHL players, 6 from rung 2 and 14 from the Extraliga112.

Tests and measured error

The repository's suite of data contracts per fetcher (fields, types, row counts per season), linking and snapshot-hash checks and analysis tests on fixtures runs at d1f9f88 with 247 software tests passing and 16 skipped; the outputs record the SHA-256 of the snapshot files they were built from, and the tests fail when the snapshot changes without a rebuild13. The per-head ranks hold under other thresholds: counting any NHL game, Czechia has 29 players, 2.66 per million, still 4th; with a flat 20 games everywhere, 20 and 53, 4th and 5th. In 2025/26 every qualifying NHL, Liiga and DEL player has a nationality; 22 of 365 in the National League and 4 of 365 in the SHL do not. Only 2022/23–2025/26 have every rung-2 league at least 90 % identified, so the five-league series before 2022/23 is a lower bound. The break model runs four chains of 1 000 draws after 1 000 tuning steps with seed 42; over the three fits the largest R-hat is 1.007 and there is one divergent transition, in Finland's1.

Limits

The KHL supplied 25 % of Czech roster spots since 2010, so the pool counted here is smaller than the one the national team drew on. AHL seasons appear only in NHL players' career records, a survivor-biased view. For 649 of the 867 Czech players on the pool page the only evidence of nationality is the Extraliga foreigner flag or a licence, which is eligibility, not citizenship. The National League and DEL publish no birth dates, so Switzerland and Germany drop out of the age medians. SHL, Extraliga, National League and DEL records without a birth date are linked on the name alone; unmatched and ambiguous counts are in the atlas's methodology. A per-million rate moves a lot for a small nation when a few players change. The atlas predicts nothing, and its counts measure presence, not quality.

Install and reproduce

From a clean clone, uv sync && make restore-snapshot puts the committed snapshot in place, make verify-snapshot checks its hashes, make analysis recomputes outputs/ (about six minutes, mostly the break model) and make pages renders the report and the pool page; no fetching is needed, and make test runs the tests13. The live atlas is at hockey.bsandova.com.
