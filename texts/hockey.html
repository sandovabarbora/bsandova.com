Player Pool Atlas, hockey: the Czech player pool against five countries

Overwhelmed? Here's the short version
  • The atlas is a public build that places every player in the pool on two maps of playing profile, one with and one without an adjustment for league strength, and overlays the national-team cycle; this page documents its method and the Czech numbers.
  • Finland has roughly four times the Czech density of NHL players and Sweden five times. The Czech forwards aged 26–29 score more points per game than any other cell in the cohort tables, but there are four of them.
  • The counts cover one season and many cells hold one or two players, so a single trade or injury moves them. The league-strength factors are approximations, and only two of them were stress-tested.
  • The atlas describes where the pool is thin. It does not say why, predicts nothing, recommends no roster and leaves out the KHL and every team's internal data.

What the atlas does and comparable work

The atlas asks where the Czech professional pool is thin compared with Finland, Sweden, Slovakia, Canada and the USA, by position and age, and what the players in it look like as producers. Counts of NHL players by birthplace are published routinely, for example on Hockey-Reference1, and league-equivalence factors that translate scoring between leagues go back at least to Desjardins2. The atlas adds a per-head count split by position and age against named peers, and a map of every player in the NHL, Liiga and Extraliga pool in one reproducible build, published at hockey.bsandova.com3.

Method

The per-head count takes every player born in a country (birthCountry in the NHL Stats API) who played at least ten NHL games in 2025/26 by MoneyPuck's totals, and divides by population45. The populations (Czechia 10.9 million) are rounded figures for about 2024 written into the code with no source recorded. Age cohorts use the age a player reaches in 2025, 2025 minus birth year: 18–21 (labelled U22), 22–25, 26–29 and 30 or older.

The mapped pool of 385 players combines three routes. NHL players count as Czech when born in Czechia, the same rule as the per-head count, so Czech players born abroad are missed in both. Liiga players count by the league's nationality field. Extraliga players enter the pool by league, because hokej.cz does not publish birth country; they are marked Czech-eligible only when they match a Czech IIHF roster, so the pool includes imports. In all, 78 players are confirmed eligible3678.

Every player is a four-dimensional vector of goals, assists and penalty minutes per game and age, projected with PCA and clustered with K-means, with the number of clusters chosen by silhouette score from 4–8 for forwards and 3–6 for defencemen. The silhouette values are not reported, and because age is one of the four features, the clusters mix playing profile with career stage. Per-game rates for players with few games are shrunk towards the league median with a fixed prior of \(K = 10\) games, not estimated from the data:

$$\hat r = \frac{\text{events} + K\,\tilde r_{\text{league}}}{\text{GP} + K}$$

At one game the median carries about 91 % of the weight and at eighty games the player's own record about 89 %; a player with five points in four games, 1.25 raw, drops to 0.59. The quality map multiplies each rate by a league factor, NHL 1.00, AHL 0.55, SHL 0.45, Liiga 0.42, NL 0.40, Extraliga 0.35 and first league 0.20, set by hand with public NHL-equivalency work as a guide29.

Worked example: Czechia in 2025/26

Fifteen Czech-born players meet the rule, 1.38 per million, fifth of six. The USA has the most NHL players in absolute terms and 335 million people to divide them by, the figure in the code. Slovakia, with half the Czech population, ranks above Czechia3.

Split by position and age, each cell gives the number of players with at least ten games and their median points per game. A median in a cell of one or two players is those players' own value and is not comparable across cells.

The youngest Czech forward cohort is one player, Kulich, against three for Finland and seven for Sweden. The 26–29 cohort of four, among them Pastrňák, Nečas and Zacha, has a median of 1.06 points per game, the highest in these tables. On defence Czechia has three NHL players in all, against nine for Finland and thirty for Sweden, and none aged 18–21. The fifteen players are the twelve forwards and three defencemen in these tables3.

The two maps show the league adjustment openly. In the style map, on raw rates, Pastrňák and Červenka sit in the same production zone, because each produces alike in his own league; in the quality map Pastrňák moves to PC1 ≈ 8.5 and Červenka to about 2. Of the 81 players who played the 2024 or 2025 World Championship for Czechia, 42 are in the mapped pool, from both the NHL and the Extraliga3.

Tests and sensitivity

The repository holds 51 software unit tests, and every stochastic step uses seed 429. The Liiga and Extraliga factors were each moved by ±20 % and the quality ranking recomputed; the NHL is the reference at 1.00 and the other factors were not varied. Moving every factor by the same percentage cannot change a ranking, so those two scenarios are not shown. In the four scenarios below the top ten changes by at most one player and the four NHL forwards at the top stay there, but the mean rank shift in the top twenty reaches 4.35 places when the Liiga factor falls, so the lower part of the ranking depends on the factors3.

Limits

Two analyses were dropped before publishing. An age curve for the Extraliga showed players improving into their thirties, because those still playing at 33 are the ones good enough to stay, so it measured survivorship. A youth-to-Extraliga conversion rate was right-censored: the older youth rosters had not been collected and the recent players had not had time to convert3.

The counts are one season of small numbers, and the 2024/25 counts, which the build fetched, are not shown here because the fetched data were not kept. Birth country misses Czech players born abroad in the NHL, and the Extraliga part of the pool includes imports. The KHL is left out for sanctions and data-access reasons, and the number of Czech players this removes was not measured. The population figures have no recorded source. The league factors are judgement, and the clusters have no reported validation. The atlas uses public data only, without the video, conditioning data and tracking that a national team's staff holds.

Install and reproduce

The pipeline is public under MIT and runs with make install && make all, and make test runs the tests9. The published numbers come from the render of 18 May 2026. The fetched data are not committed, so a rebuild fetches the current seasons and will give different numbers. The repository also holds a Neo4j player graph, a similarity search and LLM-drafted scouting briefs, described in its README. The live atlas, in English and Czech, is at hockey.bsandova.com.
