Persistence Dynamics and Welfare Costs of Urban Public Transport Delays

A traveller who checks a live app before leaving already sees where the bus really is, yet transport economics prices unreliability as if a route's delays were independent from one trip to the next. On 121 million vehicle-location records from Prague they are not independent, and the thesis puts a price on the difference. This is the short version; the sonification Delayed All the Time is what the same records sound like.

Serial correlation in Prague's delays

The value-of-reliability literature prices travel-time variability with its unconditional standard deviation, which assumes without saying so that a route's delays are drawn independently from one trip to the next. Prague's vehicle-tracking record rejects that assumption on both modes that carry inference. Within-day delays are serially correlated, with AR(2) coefficient sums of 0.727 on bus across 691 routes and 0.715 on tram across 41, and simulated impulse responses cross half their magnitude after about thirty-three minutes. Because the delay level is far more persistent than the increment, the memory sits in a slowly moving, observable state and does not build up from accumulating shocks1.

$$d_t = \phi_1 d_{t-1} + \phi_2 d_{t-2} + \varepsilon_t,\qquad \hat\phi_1+\hat\phi_2 = 0.727\ (\text{bus}),\ 0.715\ (\text{tram})$$

What persistence does to the cost of unreliability

Persistence changes the dispersion a reliability calculation should price by making a fraction of it forecastable, and it does not compound it, so the correction the appraisal literature needs is a smaller conditional variance rather than a larger multiplier. A compounding multiplier describes a single decaying shock, whereas the surface modes behave as a level plus a short-memory autoregression. The multiplier is therefore reported only for metro, whose three route clusters deny it inference in any case1.

The thesis prices the difference with an exact decomposition of delay variance into a seasonal part, a forecastable state and unforecastable noise, in which the state dominates on metro and the residual on the surface modes. The value of real-time information, meaning the gain from watching the state instead of trusting the timetable, is 5.6 % of the commuter-tier reliability cost on bus and 2.3 % on tram. Because the tiers share their calibration constants, this share is invariant to the value of time and to the reliability ratio, the two parameters that occupy much of the appraisal literature and that are not known for the Czech context. The levels in crowns per year are not invariant, and they are reported as a bracket. At a thirty-minute horizon they come to about 204 CZK a year for a bus passenger and about 60 for a tram passenger, and to 182 and 40 when held out of sample on a date split. These figures index the dispersion made forecastable, not a welfare gain a passenger has been measured to pocket1.

The three hypotheses from the proposal

The three hypotheses were fixed in the thesis proposal before any estimation, and the data revised all three. The first predicted that infrastructure segregation would order persistence across modes. Among the two modes that carry inference, traffic exposure does not predict persistence, and the gap between bus and tram is a quarter of the smallest effect the design could reliably detect, although within a route higher incoming delay does raise persistence with the expected sign. The second hypothesis predicted that variance-based costs understate welfare through a multiplier above one, and the sign runs the other way, since conventional metrics overstate the cost borne by a traveller who observes the state. The third predicted a socioeconomic gradient in persistence. Its coefficient is indistinguishable from zero, and a simulated positive control shows that the design had the power to detect a gradient, so the null is real, but a failed placebo leaves the result conditional on education being entangled with network structure. That all three failed in their registered form is part of why the exploratory result is worth reading, because it shows that the questions could not have been re-chosen once the answers were known1.

Limits of the estimates

The dispersion priced is schedule adherence, whereas for turn-up-and-go service the passenger cares about headway regularity instead, and this caveat binds hardest on metro. The persistence coefficients are within-day objects and interday correlation is far lower, so none of this describes a commuter accumulating risk across weeks. The sample runs from 15 March to 8 September 2025 and therefore contains no Prague winter, the season a study of delay persistence would most want to see. The contribution is a method that transfers beyond Prague, since the decomposition needs only vehicle-location records and a timetable, which any operator running automatic vehicle location already has, and no stated-preference survey. Whether the figures hold elsewhere is for replication to establish1.

Why the record on this site plays the delays

The thesis also changed how this site treats delays. They are not predictable in the way a prediction pipeline hopes, and because their memory is real and observable, the right product is a live view of the state as it stands. For that reason the record on this site plays the delays instead of predicting them.
