Overwhelmed? Here's the short version
  • Measured against each brand's own anchor colour, 16 of 47 spots keep their dominant hue within 20° and 11 lie more than 100° away. Speech fills more than half the runtime in 44 of 47 spots (median 76 %).
  • The colour groups rest on hand-set cut-offs and one hand-picked anchor colour per brand, and one brand, Albert, supplies 5 of the 11 spots beyond 100°; with a 90° cut-off that group is 13.
  • A view-count prediction was checked late: one claim is indeterminate and the other not supported.
  • Every number is a descriptive count on one corpus of YouTube uploads.
  • The homepage map does not establish a shared register between brands.

Four studies

This page indexes four exploratory studies built over a few weekends in spring 2026, each with its own repository and page. They describe; none tests a hypothesis. A view-count prediction drawn from Spot Scorer's pitch was registered separately on 18 May; it is checked below. The marketing literature has studied colour as an executional cue in advertising and as a carrier of brand meaning12, music as a variable in advertising3, and the distinctive assets by which a brand is recognised4. The studies here only measure what 47 spots and 24 homepages put on screen and in text. The spots are the most recent uploads of 15 to 180 seconds with more than 10 000 views on the Czech YouTube channels of 13 brands, up to five per brand5. Two spots, Kofola's and AAA Auto's, were added by hand outside that rule, and one Lidl spot runs 14.5 seconds.

The colour study samples each spot at one frame per second (2 251 frames, near-black ones dropped), groups each frame's pixels into five dominant colours with K-means in CIE-LAB, a perceptual colour space, and measures the angle on the hue wheel between the spot's dominant chromatic hue and one hand-picked anchor colour per brand, such as T-Mobile's magenta or Lidl's blue. Sixteen spots fall within 20° of the anchor, among them all five of T-Mobile's and four of Lidl's, and eleven lie beyond 100°, five of which are Albert's (151–178°); twenty lie between. Neither cut-off has a stated rationale. At 90°, where fig. 1 draws its line, the world-led group is 13 of 47. The line is a reference, not a perceptual threshold. Pilsner Urquell's brand green covers 0.2 % of its spot's picture and AAA Auto's yellow 0.001 %5.

The sound study takes the same spots, detects speech with silero-vad, a speech detector, and estimates tempo by beat tracking and key by correlating the pitch profile with major and minor templates (Krumhansl–Schmuckler) in librosa. A spot is voice-led when more than half its runtime is speech. Music here means non-speech, non-silent sound, so it includes effects and ambience. Forty-four spots are voice-led and three music-led: Pilsner Urquell's Alfons at 86 % music and two McDonald's image spots at 77 % and 76 %, with the next spot at 42 %. Tempo is between 110 and 140 bpm in 23 of 47 spots. The key finder labels 27 of 47 minor, 57 %, which is not distinguishable from an even split (binomial p ≈ 0.4). Tempo and key estimates on speech-dominated audio are both unreliable6.

The homepage study scraped the title, description, first headings and first paragraphs of 30 brand homepages, of which 24 succeeded. The texts were turned into vectors with the language model multilingual-e5-base, projected onto a two-dimensional map with UMAP and grouped with K-means (k = 4). ČSOB, Česká spořitelna, Fio, Air Bank, Komerční banka, Generali, ČEZ and Škoda Auto fall into one cluster, but the words the study lists as setting it apart are banka, škoda, čez, fio, česká, brand and sector names (it does not say how those words were picked), so the cluster may reflect names more than tone of voice. The map's 54 points are the 24 homepages and 30 search-trend phrases, so it shows approximate neighbourhoods rather than distances. The study's own page is titled "Three templates", while its clustering uses k = 47. A fourth piece, Spot Scorer, shows any spot's colour and audio metrics against its sector median8.

The view-count prediction of 18 May

On 18 May a file committed to the hub repository named five spots, three "coherent" (Pilsner, a McDonald's promo, a Lidl brand-colour spot) and two "muddled" (Albert, a Vodafone spot). The file's labels do not match the published metrics: it puts the Albert spot at 174° and the Vodafone spot at 15°, where the metrics give 151° and 1°, so the "muddled" Vodafone spot is brand-led in fig. 1. Claim A was that over thirty days, to 17 June, the coherent set would gain more YouTube views in total; claim B, that it would hold the top three ranks. The file listed the confounds (existing views, subscribers, paid promotion, the algorithm) and n = 5. The check was not run on 17 June; it was run on 14 September, 119 days after the baseline, so it is not the registered test910.

The Lidl spot was made private before the check. Claim A is indeterminate: the check ran at 119 days instead of the registered 30, so the 30-day increments were never measured. The two measurable coherent spots gained 3 813 views against 3 210 for the muddled pair, and Lidl's missing increment could only add to the coherent side, but over the wrong window. Claim B is not supported: Pilsner gained 44 views and ranks last, whatever the Lidl figure would have been. The check output is not committed (private; not verifiable). As the file itself anticipated, view counts reflect much more than the spot10.

What these studies do not show

The corpus is selected by what 13 brands upload to YouTube, not by television airtime; two spots were added by hand, and some McDonald's uploads carry Slovak-language titles (the audio language was not checked). Eight brands supply 40 of the 47 spots, so a single brand can dominate a group, as Albert does with 5 of the 11 world-led spots. Sampling at one frame per second misses flash cuts, K-means with k = 5 is a choice, anchor colours are one hand-picked value per brand, and hue distance is poorly defined for near-grey palettes such as AAA Auto's. None of the counts carries an interval. The homepage map rests on 24 of 30 pages and 54 points, and neither Spot Scorer nor any metric here has been validated against an outcome57.
