Claims, checked · Prague · started 15 September 2026 · a second use of the surf verifier

Weather, checked.

The surf verification has one weakness it admits: its truth is another model's analysis. Prague has thermometers. The Czech Hydrometeorological Institute publishes every station's daily maximum, minimum and rainfall as open data the morning after, so the same machinery can be pointed at a forecast people actually plan around, and scored against what was measured. This is that: four models, two stations, one line a day, and a page that shows a number only once there are enough days to mean one.

Why this is a better test than the surf one

On the surf page, forecast and truth come from the same family of wave models, so a day on which both are wrong together looks like a hit. Here the truth is a rain gauge and a thermometer at a station that has been measuring since 1938 (Ruzyně) and 1775 (Klementinum). A model that says 24 °C and a station that reads 20 °C disagree in a way nobody can argue with. The cost is one day of lag, since ČHMÚ publishes a day's values after it ends; the benefit is that "skill" on this page means skill against the world1.

The engineering detail that decides whether the comparison is fair

A daily maximum is not a daily maximum until you say which 24 hours. ČHMÚ's daily TMA and TMI are read at 20:00 UTC and cover the 24 hours before; its daily precipitation SRA is read at 06:00 UTC and covers the 24 hours before that. Open-Meteo's daily values run midnight to midnight local time. Compared directly, a warm evening or an overnight shower would land on different days on the two sides, and the error would be an artefact of the calendar. So the collector does not use Open-Meteo's daily values at all: it fetches hourly temperature and precipitation, including the previous day so the first window is complete, and aggregates them into the station's own windows, maximum and minimum over (D−1 20:00, D 20:00], sum over (D−1 06:00, D 06:00]. The aggregation has a test of its own with a synthetic hourly series, because it is the kind of thing that is wrong quietly2.

Two more things the page states rather than hides. Open-Meteo's best_match resolves to ICON for Prague, so on this page it is a duplicate of the ICON column and the four models are effectively three; it is kept because it is what a user of the API gets by default. And the climatology is the station's own 1991–2020 record, ±15 days around the day of year, computed once from ČHMÚ's historical file (72 MB for Ruzyně, 149 MB for Klementinum) and stored as 366 rows; it gives every day a base rate for rain and a mean to beat for temperature before a single forecast has been verified2.

What is scored, and against what

Per station, model and lead day 0–6: mean absolute error, bias and RMSE of the daily maximum, MAE and bias of the minimum, MAE of precipitation. Rain as a binary event at 1 mm: hits, misses, false alarms, probability of detection and false-alarm ratio per model; and, treating the share of models that call rain as a probability, the Brier score with skill against two baselines, the climatological rain frequency for that day of year and "rain yesterday". Temperature gets the same two baselines: the climatological mean and yesterday's observation. Intervals from a block bootstrap by ISO week, 500 resamples. Nothing is shown under 14 pairs; under 30 it is marked descriptive. The data-quality block counts issues stored against expected, nulls per model, gaps in the observation series and the share of days the models disagree by more than 4 °C at lead 12, 3.

$$\mathrm{BSS}_{\text{ref}} = 1 - \frac{\frac{1}{N}\sum_i (f_i - o_i)^2}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_i (f^{\text{ref}}_i - o_i)^2},\qquad f_i = \frac{\#\{\text{models calling} \ge 1\ \text{mm}\}}{4},\quad f^{\text{clim}}_i = p_{1991\text{–}2020}(\text{doy}_i)$$

Three predictions, dated 15 September 2026

Written before the first pair exists, so they can be wrong in public. One: at lead 1 the daily-maximum MAE will be between 1.0 and 1.6 °C at Ruzyně and larger at Klementinum, which sits in the city's heat island and is systematically warmer than the models' grid cell; the sign of the Klementinum bias will be negative (models too cold). Two: rain skill against climatology will be clearly positive at leads 1–3 and will fall to about zero by lead 6, and persistence will be a harder baseline than climatology at lead 1 and an easier one from lead 3. Three: ECMWF will have the lowest temperature MAE at leads 3–6, but not at lead 1, where ICON's finer grid will be at least as good. Each will be checked on this page when the gates open, and the result stays here either way2.

What runs it

A GitHub Actions cron at 06:40 UTC runs weather/collect.py (Open-Meteo, ČHMÚ current and previous month files, idempotent upsert by station and date) and weather/verify.py, commits the data and the page's data.json, which triggers the site deploy. Append-only JSON, one file per issue date, about 3 KB a day. A missed morning is a visible gap in the quality block, not an interpolated value. Cost: zero. The whole thing is 350 lines and five tests, and it exists because the surf verifier was built as a verifier, not as a surf page2.
