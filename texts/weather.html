Claims, checked · Prague · started 15 September 2026 · a second use of the surf verifier

Weather, checked.

The surf verification admits one weakness, which is that its truth is another model's analysis. Prague has thermometers, and the Czech Hydrometeorological Institute publishes every station's daily maximum, minimum and rainfall as open data the morning after, so the same machinery can be pointed at a forecast people actually plan around and scored against what was measured. This page does that with four models and two stations, adds one line a day, and shows a number only once there are enough days for it to mean something.

Station measurements as the answer key

On the surf page, forecast and truth come from the same family of wave models, so a day on which both are wrong together looks like a hit. Here the truth is a rain gauge and a thermometer at stations that have been measuring since 1938 (Ruzyně) and 1775 (Klementinum), and a model that says 24 °C disagrees with a station that reads 20 °C in a way nobody can argue with. The cost is one day of lag, since ČHMÚ publishes a day's values after the day ends, and in return "skill" on this page means skill against the world1.

Matching the stations' 24-hour windows

A daily maximum is only defined once it is said which 24 hours it covers. ČHMÚ's daily TMA and TMI are read at 20:00 UTC and cover the 24 hours before, and its daily precipitation SRA is read at 06:00 UTC and covers the 24 hours before that. Open-Meteo's daily values run midnight to midnight local time. Compared directly, a warm evening or an overnight shower would land on different days on the two sides, and the error would be an artefact of the calendar. The collector therefore does not use Open-Meteo's daily values at all. It fetches hourly temperature and precipitation, including the previous day so that the first window is complete, and aggregates them into the station's own windows, maximum and minimum over (D−1 20:00, D 20:00] and the sum over (D−1 06:00, D 06:00]. The aggregation has a test of its own on a synthetic hourly series, because an error in it would pass unnoticed2.

Open-Meteo's best_match resolves to ICON for Prague, so here it duplicates the ICON column and the four models are effectively three; it is kept because it is what a user of the API gets by default. The climatology is the station's own 1991–2020 record, ±15 days around the day of year, computed once from ČHMÚ's historical file (72 MB for Ruzyně, 149 MB for Klementinum) and stored as 366 rows. It gives every day a base rate for rain and a mean to beat for temperature before a single forecast has been verified2.

Scores, baselines and intervals

For each station, model and lead day 0–6 the page scores the mean absolute error, bias and RMSE of the daily maximum, the MAE and bias of the minimum, and the MAE of precipitation. Rain is also scored as a binary event at 1 mm, with hits, misses, false alarms, probability of detection and false-alarm ratio per model. Treating the share of models that call rain as a probability gives a Brier score, with skill against two baselines, the climatological rain frequency for that day of year and "rain yesterday". Temperature gets the same two baselines, the climatological mean and yesterday's observation. Intervals come from a block bootstrap by ISO week with 500 resamples. Nothing is shown under 14 pairs, and under 30 a number is marked descriptive. The data-quality block counts issues stored against expected, nulls per model, gaps in the observation series and the share of days on which the models disagree by more than 4 °C at lead 12, 3.

$$\mathrm{BSS}_{\text{ref}} = 1 - \frac{\frac{1}{N}\sum_i (f_i - o_i)^2}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_i (f^{\text{ref}}_i - o_i)^2},\qquad f_i = \frac{\#\{\text{models calling} \ge 1\ \text{mm}\}}{4},\quad f^{\text{clim}}_i = p_{1991\text{–}2020}(\text{doy}_i)$$

Three predictions, dated 15 September 2026

The predictions were written before the first pair existed, so that they can be wrong in public. The first is that at lead 1 the daily-maximum MAE will be between 1.0 and 1.6 °C at Ruzyně and larger at Klementinum, which sits in the city's heat island and is systematically warmer than the models' grid cell, and that the sign of the Klementinum bias will be negative (models too cold). The second is that rain skill against climatology will be clearly positive at leads 1–3 and will fall to about zero by lead 6, and that persistence will be a harder baseline than climatology at lead 1 and an easier one from lead 3. The third is that ECMWF will have the lowest temperature MAE at leads 3–6 but not at lead 1, where ICON's finer grid will be at least as good. Each will be checked on this page when the gates open, and the result stays here either way2.

The daily job

A GitHub Actions cron at 06:40 UTC runs weather/collect.py (Open-Meteo, ČHMÚ current and previous month files, idempotent upsert by station and date) and weather/verify.py, then commits the data and the page's data.json, which triggers the site deploy. The data are stored as append-only JSON, one file per issue date, about 3 KB a day, and a missed morning shows as a visible gap in the quality block rather than as an interpolated value. Running it costs nothing. The whole thing is 350 lines and five tests, and it exists because the surf verifier was built as a general verifier rather than as a surf page2.
