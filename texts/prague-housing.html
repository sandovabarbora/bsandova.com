Prague, measured · Part 4 of 5 · new homes in Prague, Vienna and Warsaw, 2012–2024

Prague builds two homes for Vienna's three

Three capitals of similar size, counted by their own statistical offices. Since 2015 Warsaw has completed 10.4 new homes a year for every 1 000 residents, Vienna 6.6 in new buildings alone, Prague 4.5. Prague has not passed 5 in any year since 2012. What sets Vienna apart is not only how much it builds, but who builds it.

Prague has 1.4 million residents, Vienna 2.0 million, Warsaw 1.9 million. All three argue about housing. This text puts one number next to the argument, from each country's statistical office: how many new homes the city completes in a year, for every thousand people who live there123.

How many

Prague's line is the flattest, and the lowest in every year but 2016: between 3.1 homes per thousand residents in 2013 and 4.9 in 2022, 6 489 homes in 2024. Vienna climbed through the 2010s to 8.5 in 2021, 16 262 homes in new buildings in one year, and has fallen since, to 5.4 in 2024, still above Prague. Warsaw has built more than both in every year of the series, with a peak of 13.2 in 2018, 23 430 homes. Over the ten years to 2024, Prague averaged 4.5, Vienna 6.6 and Warsaw 10.4: for every home Prague completed per resident, Vienna completed one and a half and Warsaw more than two.

Who builds

In 2024 a quarter of Vienna's new homes, 3 107 of 12 478, were built by limited-profit housing associations, companies whose profits Austrian law restricts, and another 2.4 % by the public sector itself. In Warsaw the city built 96 homes and its public building societies none, 0.7 % of the year's 14 873. Almost all of Warsaw's volume, 99.3 %, comes from builders other than the city. Prague's cannot be split the same way from the published series, so the comparison stops at volume for Prague.

The long version

A pre-registered follow-up puts Prague among 208 European metropolitan regions counted by one census, asks how long a permitted Prague home takes to get a house number, and asks whether the city builds near its metro. Two homes for Vienna's three, measured again.

What was checked

Each city is counted by its own office with its own definition, and the three differ in one way that matters: Vienna's figure leaves out homes made by converting or extending buildings until 2024, which Prague's and Warsaw's include, so Vienna is if anything understated here. Prague's figures are the Czech Statistical Office's own per-thousand rates; its first release for 2024 gave 6 511 completed homes, revised to 6 489 in the current series. Warsaw's population is Statistics Poland's end-of-year count, averaged over consecutive years, and it is revised upward from 2020 after the 2021 census. Warsaw's rates computed this way match the office's own published rate where it exists (11.50 and 11.56 for 2016 and 2017). Every number is produced by tools/praha/cities.py.
