The parts

  1. Part 1 · where delay appears · 618 tram segments, 15 March – 8 September 2025

    Where is delay born on Prague's trams?

    A tenth of the segments record 50.6 % of all delay gained, just over half, the same ones in odd and even weeks. Described afterwards, not registered: many of them leave large stops, and part of what they record is early trams waiting for their time.

  2. Part 2 · bunching · 243 465 pairs of trams, 15 March – 8 September 2025

    How often do two 22s come at once? Tram bunching in Prague, 2025

    The spacing between two trams of a line shows practically no amplification from stop to stop (−0.0012 per stop, inconclusive by the registered rule, corrected in its version 2), and bunches build gradually towards the end of a line. The rare sudden closings concentrate on some platforms, as other rare one-stop events do, and partly by line. City buses show a small positive value (+0.0021, supported), about the size of the spread among the tram estimates.

  3. Part 3 · transfers · tram-to-tram connections, 15 March – 8 September 2025

    Will you make your connection?

    Planned three minutes apart, a connection was made 80 times in a hundred and cost over five minutes more 22 % of the time. The data show a small association between the two lines' delays, about +0.6 percentage points (+0.64 registered, not supported; +0.1 to +1.2 across specifications), with real differences between pairs of lines and no mechanism identified. A roulette gives the share made for 6 624 combinations of stop, lines and time of day.

The series sits next to Weather on the rails, which describes how the same trams' delays differ in rain and heat.
