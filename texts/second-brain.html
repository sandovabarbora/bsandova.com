Knowledge engineering · 2026 · private repository

A wiki that an agent keeps

Not retrieval. Retrieval re-derives knowledge from raw documents on every question and nothing accumulates. Here the agent reads a source once, extracts what matters, and integrates it into a persistent, interlinked wiki: updating pages, strengthening claims, flagging contradictions. Obsidian is the IDE, the LLM is the programmer, the wiki is the codebase.

How it works

Three layers with different owners. Raw sources are mine and immutable; the agent reads them and never edits them. The wiki is the agent's: it creates, updates and prunes pages. The schema, the file that tells the agent what a page is, how to link, when to split, is co-evolved by both of us as we learn what works1. An index is generated from the pages rather than maintained by hand, and a log records every change, append-only. The pattern is Karpathy's LLM-wiki idea with a schema adapted from an open Obsidian second-brain layout23.

Ingesting one source means reading it, writing a summary, updating five to fifteen existing pages, updating the index, appending to the log, and reporting what was touched and what contradicted what. Contradictions between sources are recorded on the page, never smoothed into a consensus, and coverage gaps have to be marked explicitly so they can be closed later from a different source1.

What it found that I did not

Two things from the standards-and-regulation shelf. NIST AI 600-1 frames memorisation-driven data leakage without mentioning decoding parameters anywhere, while the setting most likely to leak fine-tuned data is a decoding parameter. And the EU GPAI Code of Practice asks for reproducibility in one appendix and forbids reproducing training data in another. Neither is a contradiction inside one document; both only show up when two documents are on the same page with a link between them4.

The quotation that was not there

A quotation survived several passes because it matched what the brief was looking for. It was fabricated. The fix was a tool, not a rule: quote.py prints the surrounding sentence from the source's own text for every quotation on a page, so a quote either has a neighbourhood or it does not, and the fabrication is a regression test for it5. The wiki also lints itself: orphan pages, broken links, sprawl, stale seeds, index drift, all machine-checked.

One build ran thirteen subagents in parallel and then had the wiki audit its own construction. The verdict, written by a page that had been compiled that morning from a database textbook, was that the single-writer discipline was correct and one layer too shallow. I have not had a better code review.
