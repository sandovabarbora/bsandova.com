Prague, measured · Part 3 of 5 · city-district budgets, 2022–2024

One city, 57 budgets

Prague runs on two levels: the city, and 57 districts with their own mayors, councils and budgets. Put the districts' accounts side by side, per resident, and the thirteen large ones, home to three Praguers in four, spend between 8 346 and 17 604 crowns a year. Praha 1 spends 35 320. Most of it is money they are given, not money they raise.

The city of Prague keeps the big jobs: public transport, the main roads, the city police. Its 57 districts, from Praha 4 with 135 712 residents to Praha-Nedvězí with 381, run much of daily life: most primary schools and kindergartens, local streets and green spaces, part of social services. Each reports its accounts to the Ministry of Finance, which publishes them in MONITOR1. This text reads the actual spending and income of every district for 2022, 2023 and 2024, divides it by the district's residents at the end of each year2, and averages the three years, because one school built in one year can triple a small district's budget.

Per resident

The large districts sit close together, and at the bottom of the whole city: Praha 4, the largest, spends 8 346 crowns per resident, Praha 13 9 030, Praha 2 at the top of the group 17 604. The small districts on the edge spread from about 8 000 to 35 000, and their figures are made by single projects: Praha-Lysolaje, 1 639 residents, spent 119 531 crowns per resident in 2022 and 27 118 in 2024; 73 % of its three years' spending was investment. The median district spends 14 669.

Praha 1 is the exception that does not move. It spent 35 602, 34 564 and 35 794 crowns per resident in the three years, four times Praha 4, every year.

Where the money comes from

A district raises little on its own. Transfers from the city and the state are 76 % of the median district's income, and 38–92 % across the 57. Praha 1 raises more than any: 9 676 crowns per resident in taxes and local fees against Praha 4's 1 363. Much of it is tourism. In 2024 Praha 1 collected 82.9 million crowns in the fee on overnight stays and 114.2 million for the use of public space, up from 47.5 and 97.3 million in 2022. Praha 1 also takes in 12 308 crowns per resident in rents, sales and other income of its own, against Praha 4's 905, and 13 655 in transfers, against Praha 4's 6 804. Praha 1 and Praha 2 are the only districts where transfers are less than half of income. How the city divides its money between the districts is set by its own rules and resolutions; the accounts show the result, not the formula.

What it is spent on

In the thirteen large districts the town hall itself, the section that covers administration, the council and the office, takes a median 29 % of spending, from 24 % to 38 %. Education, above all the district's kindergartens and primary schools, takes 32 %, from 18 % to 42 %. Social services take 7.5 %, from 2 % to 17 %, and investment of every kind 28 %, from 14 % to 48 %. The same service can sit in different places in different districts' accounts, so the shares compare the districts' books as much as their priorities.

The long version

A pre-registered follow-up reads every grant the city gave its districts during the year, 2015–2023, and asks what this text could not: whether a district receives more when its mayor's party joins the city's governing coalition. Does alignment pay?

What was checked

The Ministry's bulk extract of municipal accounts leaves the Prague districts out; their figures come from MONITOR's public interface, which serves the same reports1. Income and spending include the money a district moves between its own accounts; own-fund transfers (items 4131, 4132, 4140) are counted as the district's own revenue, not as transfers. Spending sections are grouped by name, because the same one, education, is coded 31 in most districts' 2024 accounts and 32 in eleven. The 57 districts' residents add up to 1 397 880, Prague's population at the end of 20242. Every number is produced by tools/praha/districts.py.
