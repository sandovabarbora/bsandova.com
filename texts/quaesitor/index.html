Quaesitor: silent failure in AI answers over data warehouses

Three texts from a retired one-person service that asked AI assistants questions with known answers over five warehouses and counted how often they returned a wrong number without any warning.

answers
5 794 one per question and repeat in 125 run reports, 11–25 August 2026; 9 unmeasured (the model call failed) · 15 languages · administrative count
failure modes catalogued
26 each planted in a warehouse built for it · administrative count
median error, without → with documentation
72 % → 23 % 22 questions, one model, Czech · exact sign test p = 0.0023 · no interval
Overwhelmed? Here's the short version
  • Text 1 reports that two Claude models with the same number of correct answers on one pack differed sharply in how often they declined, and that schema documentation made errors smaller without making them rarer.
  • Text 2 lists the planted failure modes with the median size of the error each produced; about a third of them produced errors under 10 %, small enough to look plausible.
  • Text 3 gives the method and eight results by other authors, including a benchmark in which a semantic layer raised accuracy by 17 to 23 points.
  • All three are exploratory: the warehouses are synthetic or small, the rates carry no intervals, and the run reports sit in a private repository, so a reader can check the published tables but not rebuild them.
