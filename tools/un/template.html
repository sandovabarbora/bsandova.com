Research · politics · Czechia at the UN, 2014–2025

Czechia's votes at the UN: did agreement with the EU change between governments?

In recorded votes of the UN General Assembly, Czechia voted with the majority of the other EU members on {{cz_lo}} to {{cz_hi}} % of votes in the periods of each of its last three governments, as most members did. Relative to the other members, its agreement changed by {{a_est}} points from the Sobotka to the Babiš period and by {{b_est}} points from the Babiš to the Fiala period, and neither change meets the registered rule for a shift. The question about the third Babiš government is registered and waits for the votes of late 2026.

In the UN General Assembly, member states vote on resolutions that range from nuclear disarmament to the budget of the UN's agency for Palestine refugees. Most texts pass without a vote. When a state asks for one, every member's vote is recorded, and the UN Dag Hammarskjöld Library publishes these records as a data file1. The EU members coordinate their positions in New York and usually vote alike, so how often a member votes with the rest of the EU is a plain measure of where it stands among them. Research on these records more often places states on a scale estimated from all of their votes2; this study keeps to agreement, which can be read vote by vote.

Over the years this stage covers, Czechia had three governments: Bohuslav Sobotka's from January 20143, Andrej Babiš's first and second from December 201745, and Petr Fiala's from December 2021 to {{cab_25}}, when Andrej Babiš's third government took over6. The study asks whether Czechia's agreement with the EU majority, and with the United States, Russia, Hungary and Slovakia, changed between the periods of these governments by more than the other EU members' agreement changed over the same votes. A change measured that way is an association with a period. A new government comes with a new foreign minister, new officials and a world agenda that moves on its own, and none of these is separated here.

The design was registered before any Czech vote in the data was read. This page reports its first stage: the two earlier changes, in 2017 and in 2021, and the votes on which Czechia left the EU majority. The headline question, about the third Babiš government, is tested on votes cast from {{q1_from}} to {{q1_to}}, after registration, and is still open.

Data

The file is version 5 of the UN's General Assembly Voting Data, published in February 2026, with one row for each member's vote on each resolution adopted by a recorded vote1. From {{sob_from}} to the end of the file on 30 December 2025 it holds {{votes_all}} recorded votes. Votes on single paragraphs, amendments and drafts that failed are not in it, so the study sees adopted resolutions only. A position is yes, no or abstain; a member that did not vote has no position on that vote. The EU is the 27 members of February 2020 in every period, and the United Kingdom is left out throughout, so that the set of members never changes.

The periods follow the cabinets, each appointed with its foreign minister, who instructs the mission in New York. Each later period starts {{buffer}} days after the appointment, because in December the plenary adopts texts that were voted in committee weeks before, under the old government. The Sobotka period runs from {{sob_from}}, the day the cabinet was appointed and the start of the data used, to {{sob_to}}: {{sob_votes}} votes, on {{sob_split}} of which the other EU members did not all vote alike. The Babiš period runs from {{bab_from}} to {{bab_to}} ({{bab_votes}} votes, {{bab_split}} split), and the Fiala period from {{fia_from}} to {{fia_to}} ({{fia_votes}} votes, {{fia_split}} split). The first Babiš cabinet, appointed on {{cab_17}}, lost its confidence vote and is pooled with the second45; the Fiala cabinet was appointed on {{cab_21}}6. The {{buf17_votes}} and {{buf21_votes}} votes in the two gaps belong to neither period.

How this was done

On each vote, Czechia's agreement with a position is 1 if it voted the same way, 0.5 if one of the two abstained and the other did not, and 0 for yes against no. The EU majority is the most common position of the other EU members that voted, and a tie drops the vote. For a partner such as Russia, the position is that partner's own vote.

The registered estimate, β, is a difference in differences on votes. On each vote, it takes Czechia's agreement minus the mean agreement of the other {{eu_others}} members, each of them scored against the majority of the members other than itself and Czechia; β is the mean of that difference over the later period minus its mean over the earlier one. A β of −1 point means that Czechia's agreement fell by one point more, or rose by one point less, than the other members' agreement over the same votes. A change in what the Assembly votes on, or in a partner's own votes, therefore shows in every member and is not counted as a Czech change. Intervals come from resampling agenda items, the families of resolutions that return every year, within each period ({{draws}} draws).

The same β is computed with each other EU member in Czechia's place. The rank p is the share of all members, Czechia included, whose β divided by its standard error is at least as far from zero as Czechia's. With {{eu_n}} members, a p of {{p_cut}} or less puts Czechia among the two or three members whose change stands out most.

Each β takes the first label that applies. It is lower or higher agreement after the change if β is at least {{minimum}} points from zero, its 95 % interval excludes zero and p is at most {{p_cut}}; no change larger than {{margin}} points if its 90 % interval lies within ±{{margin}} points; underpowered if the 90 % interval is wider than {{margin2}} points, so that the previous rule could not have applied; and inconclusive otherwise. The margin was set from a power analysis run on the other members only, before any Czech vote was read: for changes like the two of stage A, the median member's 90 % interval was {{pw_q2_width}} points wide, and nine in ten members moved by less than {{pw_q2_beta}} points relative to the rest.

Checks, partners and the numbers of members

Seven checks were registered with the estimate and are reported without relabelling it: β on split votes only, where the other EU members did not all vote alike; no half point for an abstention; after periods from the cabinet date with no buffer; a comparison without Hungary and Slovakia, whose own governments changed within the window; regular sessions only, without the emergency special sessions; after periods from the confidence votes of {{conf_17}} and {{conf_21}} instead of the appointments; and, for the United States, the Fiala period cut at {{us_change}}, when the US government changed. Czechia's share of votes it did not cast is reported per period, because a government that avoids votes would be invisible to β. A cross-check against UNGA-DM, a database of General Assembly decisions compiled independently of the UN file7, tests the positions themselves.

When the partner is Hungary or Slovakia, that member is neither in Czechia's place nor among the comparison members, so its row ranks {{ranked_partner}} members instead of {{ranked_eu}}. Other members are ranked only if they voted on at least {{min_after}} votes of the later period. The partners give twelve labels over the three changes, eight of them in this stage, reported without a correction for multiplicity; none is read on its own.

Agreement with the EU majority in 2017 and 2021

Czechia voted with the majority of the other EU members on {{cz_sob}} % of the votes of the Sobotka period, {{cz_bab}} % of the Babiš period and {{cz_fia}} % of the Fiala period. Over the same votes, the comparison members' mean was {{co_sob}}, {{co_bab}} and {{co_fia}} %. Czechia thus went from slightly above the other members' mean to slightly below it, by less than the uncertainty of the estimate. For the change of 2017, β is {{a_est}} points (90 % interval {{a_ci90}}, 95 % {{a_ci95}}; rank p {{a_p}} of {{a_m}}), labelled {{a_label}}. For the change of 2021, β is {{b_est}} points (90 % {{b_ci90}}, 95 % {{b_ci95}}; p {{b_p}} of {{b_m}}), labelled {{b_label}} (fig. 1).

Neither estimate meets the rule for a shift, and neither rules one out. The 2017 interval reaches past −{{margin}} points, so a fall of about that size is consistent with the data, and the label is {{a_label}}. The 2021 interval is {{b_width}} points wide, more than twice the margin, against a median of {{pw_q2_width}} points for the other members in the power analysis; the rule of no change could not have applied, and the label is {{b_label}}. In 2021, Czechia's β with the EU majority was {{low_b_eu}} of the {{b_m}} members' and its β with Russia {{low_b_rus}}. The rank p measures each β against its own uncertainty, and Czechia's was larger than most: a standard error of {{se_b_eu}} points against a median of {{se_b_med}} for the other members, which fits departures concentrated in a few agenda items that return every year. Among the other members, the change that stood out most relative to its own uncertainty was {{stand_a}}'s in 2017, at {{stand_a_est}} points, and {{stand_b}}'s in 2021, at {{stand_b_est}} points.

A yearly view, drawn after the results and not registered, shows the same levels without subtracting the other members' change (fig. 2). Each member is scored against the most common position of the other 26. Czechia's agreement ranged from {{yr_lo}} % to {{yr_hi}} % per calendar year and stayed inside the range of the other members every year. It was below their median in every year from {{yr_first_below}} to {{yr_last}}, at its lowest place {{yr_rank_worst}} of 27 in {{yr_rank_worst_years}}. Czechia can be above the comparison members' mean while below their median because a few members, most often {{low_members}}, agree with the majority much less than the rest and pull the mean down.

The United States, Russia, Hungary and Slovakia

Relative to them, β was {{ea_rus}} points in 2017 ({{la_rus}}, p {{pa_rus}}) and {{eb_rus}} points in 2021 ({{lb_rus}}). With Hungary, Czech agreement was {{cs_hun}}, {{cb_hun}} and {{cf_hun}} %, against the other members' {{os_hun}}, {{ob_hun}} and {{of_hun}} %, so β was {{ea_hun}} and {{eb_hun}} points ({{la_hun}}, {{lb_hun}}). Votes on which Czechia left the EU majority

The registered list holds every vote on which Czechia's position differed from the majority of the other EU members: {{d_sobotka}} of {{v_sobotka}} votes in the Sobotka period ({{rate_sob}} %), {{d_babis}} of {{v_babis}} in the Babiš period ({{rate_bab}} %) and {{d_fiala}} of {{v_fiala}} in the Fiala period ({{rate_fia}} %). The totals count the votes on which Czechia voted and the other members' majority was not tied. A count of departures does not subtract what the other members did; that comparison is β.

To read the list, the votes were grouped into four topics after the results had been seen, by words in the agenda item and the title, in this order: nuclear weapons and disarmament; Israel and Palestine, which includes the Golan and UNRWA, the UN agency for Palestine refugees; racism, meaning the follow-up to the Durban conference; and everything else. The rules are in tools/un/departures.py, and the table view of fig. 3 gives every vote with its topic. In the Sobotka period, {{t_sobotka_nuclear}} of the {{d_sobotka}} departures were on nuclear weapons and {{t_sobotka_racism}} on the Durban follow-up, against {{t_sobotka_israel_palestine}} on Israel and Palestine. In the Babiš period, {{t_babis_israel_palestine}} were on Israel and Palestine, {{t_babis_nuclear}} on nuclear weapons, {{t_babis_racism}} on the Durban follow-up and {{t_babis_other}} on other topics. In the Fiala period, {{t_fiala_israel_palestine}} of the {{d_fiala}} ({{fia_ip_share}} %) were on Israel and Palestine: the resolutions on Israeli settlements, on Israeli actions in occupied East Jerusalem and the rest of the occupied territory, on the question of Palestine and on UNRWA, which recur every year, and the votes of the emergency special session on the occupied Palestinian territory, among them those on Gaza.

Most departures were half a step away from the majority. In the Fiala period, Czechia abstained where most of the EU voted yes on {{ay_fiala}} votes and voted no where most abstained on {{na_fiala}}. On {{o_fiala}} votes it voted no while most of the other members voted yes, all of them on Israel and Palestine. There were {{o_sobotka}} such votes in the Sobotka period and {{o_babis}} in the Babiš period: {{bab_opp}}.

Robustness

None of the registered checks changes a label, by design; they show how much the estimates move. The largest moves come from split votes only, the votes on which a member's departures have room to show: β is {{split_a}} points for 2017 and {{split_b}} points for 2021, with 90 % intervals of {{split_a_ci}} and {{split_b_ci}}, from {{split_a_n}} and {{split_b_n}} votes in the later periods. With the United States, the Fiala period cut at {{us_change}} gives {{usa_cut}} points ({{usa_cut_ci}}). Without the emergency special sessions, which held {{es_fia}} of the Fiala period's votes, the 2021 estimate is {{reg_b}} points. The other checks stay within {{chk_dev}} points of the registered estimates.

The third Babiš government, still to be tested

The headline question is registered and not yet answered. Its β compares Czechia's agreement with the EU majority in the Fiala period, {{fia_from}} to {{fia_to}}, with the votes cast from {{q1_from}} to {{q1_to}}, net of the other members' change, with the same frozen code; the partners are compared in the same way. These votes come after registration. The votes from {{cab_25}} to registration have been cast and are public, but no Czech position among them has been read for this study; they will be listed in stage B as a separate block and not tested. Version 5 of the file, which ends in December 2025, came out in February 2026, so results are expected around February 2027. If no version covering December 2026 is out by 30 April 2027, stage B waits and the change log says so.

The result will take the labels above. With about {{pw_q1_votes_lo}} to {{pw_q1_votes_hi}} votes in one autumn, the power analysis put the median member's 90 % interval at up to {{pw_q1_width}} points, so a change of about {{margin}} points or more can be told from ordinary movement and one of a single point cannot. Lower agreement after the change would mean that in the votes of late 2026 Czechia's agreement with the EU majority fell, relative to the other members, by at least {{minimum}} points, with a 95 % interval below zero and a rank p of {{p_cut}} or less. No change larger than {{margin}} points would mean that the 90 % interval lies within ±{{margin}} points. Czech agreement in the Fiala period, {{cz_fia}} %, leaves {{headroom}} points to the ceiling, so higher agreement has little room. Underpowered and inconclusive would mean the votes cannot tell.

The author stated her expectation on 15 October 2026, before any Czech vote was read: “I expect a greater lean towards 'Trumpist' policy, and perhaps a greater lean towards Hungary, Russia and Slovakia, though with the Russian invasion of Ukraine in mind” (in Czech: „očekávám větší náklon k 'trumpistické' politice a možná větší náklon Maďarsko, Rusko, Slovensko, ale s ohledem na ruskou invazi na Ukrajinu“). Read against the questions, it expects higher agreement with the United States, Hungary and Slovakia, and perhaps with Russia, in the third Babiš period, which is the partner part of stage B. She stated no expectation for the EU majority or for the earlier changes, so nothing on this page tests it.

What this does not show

It does not show what a government did. A UN vote is cast by the mission on instructions, and this design cannot say who decided a vote, or whether a change came from the government, the ministry, the officials or the agenda. Only adopted resolutions are in the file, so positions on failed drafts, amendments and single paragraphs, where a member may leave the EU line more often, are not seen. Agreement with the EU majority sits near its ceiling for most members, so a change shows mostly as a handful of votes on which a member leaves an otherwise united EU, and a few votes move the estimate. The partners change too: the United States in January 2025, Slovakia in October 2023, and Hungary held a parliamentary election in April 2026, so the partner estimates mix Czech changes with theirs. The headline question rests on one autumn's votes, about {{pw_q1_votes_lo}} to {{pw_q1_votes_hi}}, and can tell only a change of a few points from ordinary movement. The grouping of departures by topic was made after the results and is a reading aid, not a test.

Reproduction

Every number on this page is filled in by a script from results.json, the screening and power files, departures.json and describe.json. From the repository root, with the UN file in tools/data/un/:

  1. tools/un/screen.py and power.py: screening and power, without Czech positions.
  2. estimate.py --stage A: the registered estimates, checks and the list of departures, with the definitions in votes.py and tests in test_un.py.
  3. departures.py and describe.py: the descriptions drawn after the results (topics, yearly levels, positions on departures).
  4. article.py: the figures and this page.
