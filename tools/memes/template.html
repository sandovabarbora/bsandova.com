Overwhelmed? Here's the short version
  • A photo of Kevin James smirking circulated as a meme, and the earliest post Know Your Meme lists is dated {{k_day0}} 2023. Over the next five weeks his English Wikipedia article had about {{k_excess}} more views than a model of his usual days expected, {{k_up}} on a typical day and {{k_ratio}} times the expected views in total, with the largest gap on {{k_peak_day}}. Only about one date in forty of his own four years showed a gap as large. After the correction for two tests the label is positive, with a corrected p of {{k_holm}} against a threshold of 0.05.
  • For Eric Stonestreet's smiling photo as Cam of Modern Family, the earliest post Know Your Meme lists is dated {{s_day0}}. His article ran {{s_up}} against his castmates' pages, a gap that most ordinary dates matched or beat. His page is so spiky that only a jump of about {{s_mdu}} on average would have been seen four times in five. The label is uninformative, which is not the same as nothing happened.
  • The two King of Queens castmates also rose, by about half as much, and the test could not rule out a shared cause. Leah Remini reacted to the meme on the day of Kevin James's largest gap. A longer, 64-day window loses significance.
  • A page view is someone looking an actor up, not someone seeing a meme. These are associations around dated posts, not effects of the memes.

The question

In the autumn of 2023 two photographs of sitcom actors went round the internet as memes. One was Kevin James, the star of The King of Queens, smirking in a stock photo. The other was Eric Stonestreet as Cameron Tucker of Modern Family, smiling. Know Your Meme dates the first known post of each on X, 21 September for Kevin James and 27 September for Stonestreet[[kym-kevin]][[kym-cam]]. The question is narrow. In the weeks around that post, did the actor's English Wikipedia article get more views than its own history says can happen by chance? A view is most often someone looking the actor up, though some automated traffic remains. So the study asks whether views of the article were higher around the posts, not whether people saw a picture.

The design, the windows, the labels and the code were fixed before any pageview number was downloaded, under signed tags in this site's repository. One choice, Kevin James's second baseline article, was made after the download and before any estimate. Each meme got one test and one of four labels: positive, inconclusive, no detectable jump, or uninformative, the last for a null from a test too weak to see a jump of 10 % on average. The two memes got different labels. Kevin James's article is labelled positive. Eric Stonestreet's is labelled uninformative.

What I measured

For each actor I took the daily views of the English article from 2021 to 2024, counting people and not the bots Wikimedia recognises[[api]]. The expected views come from a model fitted on about four months of spring and summer 2023, ending a month before the post. It predicts the actor's views from a baseline of other actors' articles and the day of the week. For Stonestreet the baseline is the median of his nine Modern Family castmates. For Kevin James it is the median of {{pool_n}} first-billed actors of other network sitcoms that ended between 2016 and 2021, plus his co-star Victor Williams. That pair was chosen before any estimate because it made the test less noisy. His other castmate stayed out of the baseline, because Leah Remini reacted to the meme herself.

The gap between actual and expected views, summed over 34 days from three days before the first post to thirty days after, is the test statistic. Whether a gap is large is judged against the same actor's other dates. The model and the sum are repeated at every date in the four years that lies well away from the meme, {{k_n}} dates for Kevin James and {{s_n}} for Stonestreet, and the p-value is the share of them with a sum at least as large. The two tests share a Holm correction[[holm]], so the smaller p has to be under 0.025. It is the event study of finance, with page views in place of share prices[[mackinlay]].

Kevin James: a rise that lasted weeks

Before the first post his article ran close to its expected level of about {{k_exp_pre}} views a day. It began to climb the day after. The gap to expected was largest on {{k_peak_day}}, day 5, with {{k_peak_views}} views against {{k_peak_exp}} expected, {{k_peak_up}}. Know Your Meme records that on the same day Leah Remini, his co-star, reacted to the memes on X[[kym-kevin]]. Whether the visitors followed her post or both followed the same wave, the data cannot say. Raw views were highest on {{k_second_day}}, with {{k_second_views}}. The daily gap stayed above the range of ordinary dates from day {{k_out_first}} to day {{k_out_run}} and once more on day {{k_out_late}}, and by day 30 it was down to {{k_last_up}} (fig. 1). Over the 34 days the article had about {{k_excess}} more views than expected. The headline {{k_up}} is the typical daily gap, averaged on a log scale. Summed over the 34 days, the page had {{k_ratio}} times its expected views.

Of {{k_n}} other dates in his own series, {{k_beat}} had a summed gap as large, a p-value of {{k_p}}. After the Holm correction for the two tests it is {{k_holm}}, just under the threshold of 0.05, so the registered label is positive. The p-value is near the resolution of the test. The dates overlap, so four years hold only about 27 separate windows, and a p-value of about 0.02 can say "among the largest" but not by how much.

Most of the registered checks agree. A shorter window of 18 days gives {{k_w14_up}} (p {{k_w14_p}}), his two King of Queens castmates as the only baseline give {{k_koq_up}} (p {{k_koq_p}}), and placebo dates from the same season of other years give p {{k_season_p}}. Among the {{k_st_n}} pool actors on the same dates, his gap ranked near the top (p {{k_st_p}}). One check does not agree, and the design says to name it next to the label. Over a window of 64 days the average is {{k_w60_up}} with p {{k_w60_p}}, above 0.05, and p {{k_w60lb_p}} again without Matt LeBlanc in the pool. The extra month adds little to the sum, which grows from {{k_car}} to {{k_w60_car}}, while ordinary dates spread wider over 64 days, so more of them reach it. The label stands on the registered window. The longer one is reported beside it.

Eric Stonestreet: nothing the test could see

Stonestreet's article tells a different story, or none. Over the 34 days from {{s_day0}} it ran {{s_up}} against the model, about {{s_excess}} fewer views than expected, and {{s_beat}} of his {{s_n}} other dates had a summed gap at least as large (p {{s_p}}). Its highest single day, {{s_peak_raw_day}}, was {{s_peak_raw}} above expected, inside the range of ordinary dates, and on no day did his page rise above that range (fig. 1). Moving day 0 to 6 October, the most-liked post Know Your Meme lists, gives {{s_d6_up}}. Leaving out Sofía Vergara, the castmate the author's spec singled out for news of her own that year, gives {{s_vg_up}}. Against the pool of outside actors instead of his castmates it is {{s_ext_up}}. The first notable TikTok of the meme, on 13 June 2023, tested on its own, gives {{s_tt_up}} (p {{s_tt_p}}).

That is not evidence that nobody looked him up. His page is spiky. In the years before, single days reached up to {{s_spike_max}} times its usual level (fig. 3), and against that noise the test could only have caught a jump of about {{s_mdu}} on average over the window four times in five. On made-up series the design had expected about {{s_mdu_design}}. Real pages are rougher than simulations, as the design warned. So the registered label is uninformative, and its sentence is plain: no jump I can tell from chance, but the test could not have seen one below about {{s_mdu}} on average over the window, so this says little.

The meme may have travelled by scrolling, liked and shared by people who never typed his name. That would leave this test empty. But a rise of 10 or 30 % would leave it empty too. The data cannot tell those apart.

Half-lives, not compared

The design also asked, without a test, how fast each rise faded: the days from the peak of the seven-day average gap until it stays under half of that peak. Kevin James's took {{k_hl}} days from its peak on {{k_hl_peak}} ({{k_hl_raw}} on the raw daily series). Stonestreet's took {{s_hl}} days, but from a peak of {{s_peak}} on {{s_peak_day}} on a series with no jump the test could see. A half-life of noise is not a half-life. The difference of {{h2_diff}} days carries no claim about which meme faded faster.

What would refute it

The obvious rival to a meme is news about the show. If the castmates' pages rose as much as the actor's, a reunion, an anniversary or a streaming release would fit the rise as well as the meme does. The design compares the median of each cast with the pool of outside actors, using the same model and dates. For Stonestreet the Modern Family cast did not rise (summed gap {{s_f_car}}, p {{s_f_p}}). No cast-wide move was seen, but the test's power is about {{s_f_power}}.

For Kevin James the two King of Queens castmates had a summed gap of {{k_f_car}} against his {{k_car}}, with p {{k_f_p}}. No cast-wide move was seen, but the test's power is about {{k_f_power}}. The registered sentence is true of the threshold, and it is not the whole picture. In the 18-day check the castmates' gap does pass the threshold (p {{k_f14_p}}), so news about the show or spillover of the meme cannot be ruled out, and Leah Remini's own reaction falls inside that window. With those two castmates as his only baseline, which would absorb a rise they shared, he still ran {{k_koq_up}} above expectation (p {{k_koq_p}}). His result holds against that baseline too.

Limits

Page views measure attention, not meme-ness. A view is mostly someone looking an actor up. People who saw the meme, laughed and scrolled on never appear here, and the filter for people cannot remove every automated visit.

Day 0 is the earliest post on X that Know Your Meme lists, not the day the meme took off. Both dates were found in that source before any data were read, so neither is exploratory, and both enter the corrected test. A date a day or two off moves little in a 34-day sum.

The baseline actors have their own news. In the months the models were fitted, Sofía Vergara's page had a day of {{vg_ratio}} times its usual views in July 2023 and Victor Williams's one of {{vw_ratio}} times in the same week. Leah Remini's page spiked in June, in August and again on 8 September, two weeks before the meme. These are listed in the repository and were not acted upon. Kevin James's views hardly moved with the pool in spring and summer 2023 (a slope of {{k_beta}}), so his expected views rest mostly on his own level and weekly rhythm in those months.

These are associations around dated posts, not effects of the memes. The two memes belong to one autumn wave of actor photos. The 2023 actors' strike and the postponed Emmy ceremony moved attention to actors in general, and other news about these actors in the same weeks cannot be excluded. The study shows that one article rose around its post far more than on ordinary dates, not that the meme made it rise. It covers two actors and one language edition, and it says nothing about memes in general.

The author's expectation

Before any number was downloaded, the author stated that she did not know what to expect, "rather b": a jump, but a small one that fades within a few days. For Kevin James the rise was neither small nor quick, {{k_up}} on average over five weeks with a half-life of {{k_hl}} days. For Stonestreet the data cannot say. A small jump that fades within days is exactly what this test could not see.

Method, details and reproduction

Method: data, model, placebo test and labels

Data

Daily views of 57 English Wikipedia articles from 1 January 2021 to 31 December 2024, all access methods, agent "user", downloaded on 26 October 2026 one article at a time[[api]]. Titles were checked through the MediaWiki API before the download. The spec's control Gary Valentine is a redirect to another person and has no page of his own, so the King of Queens cast is Leah Remini and Victor Williams. The spec's Rico Rodriguez is a disambiguation page, replaced by the actor's page linked from the Modern Family infobox. A day the API omits is kept as missing, never as zero. The reference pool is the first-billed actor of every network sitcom in four Wikipedia categories that ended between 2016 and 2021, by a written rule applied before any data, 44 actors. One, Otmara Marrero, had an omitted day and was dropped before any estimate, which leaves {{pool_n}}.

Model

Attention is log(1 + views). For each event the model regresses the target's attention on the daily median attention of the baseline and on weekday dummies, over days −150 to −31: {{est_kj}} 2023 for Kevin James, and {{est_st}} 2023 for Stonestreet less 6 June to 13 July, the weeks around the first TikTok, which leaves 82 days. The abnormal attention is the gap to the model's prediction from day −3 to day +30, the CAR is its sum and the mean uplift is exp(mean gap) − 1. For Kevin James the baseline also carries Victor Williams's attention as a second regressor, chosen in stage 1 because the placebo CAR spread was {{k_sd_b}} with it and {{k_sd_a}} without. The fitted slopes were {{k_beta}} on the pool median and {{k_beta_vw}} on Victor Williams for Kevin James, and {{s_beta}} on the cast median for Stonestreet.

Placebo test and labels

A placebo date is any date whose estimation and event windows lie in the data at least 30 days from the real window and clear of the blocked weeks, with no omitted day in its event window. Kevin James's placebo dates also avoid January and February 2024, when a later wave of his meme is recorded. Each placebo fit uses exactly as many estimation days as the real one. The p-value is (k + 1)/(n + 1), with k the placebo CARs at least as large as the real one, one-sided. Stage 1 computed the minimum detectable uplift from the placebo dates alone: the mean uplift that, injected as a jump fading with a five-day time constant, would push the CAR past the 97.5th placebo percentile four times in five. Labels take the first rule that applies: positive if the Holm p is below 0.05 and the CAR above zero, inconclusive if only the raw p is, uninformative if neither is and the minimum detectable uplift exceeds 10 %, no detectable jump otherwise.

Before the memes: the screening figure and the spikes

Stage 1 drew every target and cast article and the pool median up to 17 September 2023, day −4 of the earlier meme, before any estimate (the registered figure). Fig. 3 redraws the two targets with their baselines. Spikes were recorded, not acted upon. In Stonestreet's estimation window his own page spiked on {{st_spike_jul}} (and on 28 April, two days before the window opens), and in his baseline Sofía Vergara's a day earlier and Ed O'Neill's on 13 May. In Kevin James's window Victor Williams's page, part of his baseline, jumped on 19 July and stayed higher for weeks, and Leah Remini's, outside his baseline, spiked on 1 June and 3 August. Leah Remini's page spiked again on 8 September, between the estimation and event windows. Kevin James's own page had no spike in 2023. The full list, by a fixed rule, is in the study's README.

Disclosures and reproduction

The design discloses what was seen before registration (§0): the two Know Your Meme pages, which give the dates, Leah Remini's reaction and the later wave, the API's documentation and the titles. No pageview number and no chart of search interest was read. The design was drafted with an AI assistant, which may carry a vague memory of these memes from its training data that cannot be audited. The Rolling Stone India piece of the author's spec was not read and is not a source. The figures, the bands, the daily numbers in describe.json and this article were added after the results by tools/memes/describe.py and article.py, which reuse the frozen model and stop unless they reproduce each registered CAR and placebo percentile. No registered label was changed. Each step is dated in the change log.
