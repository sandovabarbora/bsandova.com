The two 88s, tracked daily.

Two Czech forwards wear the same number for different teams. This page follows them through the season with the whole league as the yardstick: an expected-goals model fitted on every unblocked shot, shooting rates pulled toward what forwards usually manage, and a simulated date for Pastrňák’s 1,000th point. They were picked for the number, not for anything the numbers show.

Where they shoot from

Every unblocked shot each player has taken, on the attacking half of the rink. A dot’s size is its expected-goal value (xG), the share of league shots from that spot and situation that went in. Black dots are goals. Empty-net shots are left out, and each player is scored by a model refitted without any of his own shots.

Against every shooter in the league

Goals minus xG for everyone with 30 or more unblocked shots. The band is where 80% of shooters would land if each scored at exactly the model’s rates, so dots outside it are scoring more or less than their shot locations predict.

What their shooting rate probably is

A season of shots is a small sample. Each player’s rate is blended with what 1,304 forward seasons say about shooting, a beta-binomial estimate whose weight on his own shots grows as he shoots more.

Every team against its goal difference

Points above or below what a team’s goals for and against usually earn, from a Pythagorean expectation fitted on 96 past team seasons, and 5-on-5 PDO (shooting plus save percentage, 100 is average). A gap can come from chance, goaltending, finishing or overtime.
