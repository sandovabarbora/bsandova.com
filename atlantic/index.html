The Atlantic, played · one bar a day since 14 June 2026 · from the surf verifier's own file

A record that gets a bar longer every morning.

The surf forecast verification stores one line a day for three Portuguese spots: the swell that arrived, its period, its direction, the wind. Here every day is one bar. Swell height is the note, period is the pulse inside the bar, wind is noise, direction is where it sits in the stereo field, and a day that clears the surfable rule, the same rule the verifier scores, rings a bell. Nothing is composed; the sea is. The file is fetched live, so the piece is one bar longer than it was yesterday.

How the sea is mapped

the seathe soundrange in the data
swell height, daily maxnote of the pad, D minor pentatonic over two octaves; louder as it grows0.46 – 2.76 m
mean periodpulse rate inside the bar: a 10 s groundswell pulses slowly, a 4 s windchop chatters3.5 – 10 s
mean windfiltered noise, level with speed; offshore (E ± 60°) opens the filter, onshore closes it5 – 26 km/h
swell directionstereo position: north-west left, south-west right230 – 340°
the surfable rulea bell on every day with height 1.0–2.5 m, period ≥ 8 s, wind ≤ 25 km/h or offshorethe verifier's rule, unchanged

The mapping is the whole piece. There is no melody written over it and no day is skipped; a flat week sounds like a flat week. The same rule that decides surfable on the verification page decides the bell here, and the same file feeds both, so if the collector misses a morning the bar is silent rather than invented. Delayed All the Time did this for Prague's buses; this is the same idea pointed at the sea, and the reason it was built: some things in data you hear before you see them, and a swell that holds for three days is one of them.
