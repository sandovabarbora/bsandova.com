What changed after registration

  • 1 October 2026, before this analysis was run: the tours were widened from Love On Tour to every tour since 2017 with Boxscore figures, to match the series (e890c27), and the catalogue and ticket questions were added for all five parts (12417ae).
  • 1 October 2026, after the results: countries are also ranked by days alone, because small markets with many one-day number ones inflate the registered ratio. The registered ratio and its ranking are reported unchanged (ebf819f).
  • 2 October 2026, before publication: a parsing error was corrected. The 2017–2018 tour table gives the year in each date rather than in its caption, so its 74 entries had been joined to the latest GDP per capita instead of their own year's. Only the ticket results changed; the first results commit keeps the error for comparison.
  • 3 October 2026, after an audit: Closer (2016) was found in the reference set of global number ones, kept by a parsing error that gave it a 2026 first-chart date. It breaks the registered rule that songs whose start is not seen are left out, and it makes the share of number ones that lasted longer than As It Was too high. The figures have not yet been recomputed; the error is disclosed where they appear.
  • 6 October 2026: Closer left out, as the registered rule requires (its track page is now marked in parse.py as one whose start is not seen), and every figure recomputed with the same scripts. The reference set is 164 number ones, not 165, with 30 left out, not 29, and 31 still charting, not 32. The share of number ones that lasted longer than As It Was is 4.6 % (95 % CI 1.0 % to 9.1 %), not 5.5 % (1.4 % to 10.3 %): about one in 22, not one in 18. The other own songs and the median are unchanged at the precision shown. The bootstrap intervals of the later questions (the country ratios in fig. 2 and its table, and nights against share sold) draw from the same random stream as this one, so they move slightly though their data did not change; nights against share sold out is −0.10 to 0.16 (was −0.09 to 0.16). No label changed.

Change log

Two smaller wordings corrected: songs that “stay in the chart, on and off, for years” instead of “what people play every week for years”, and the Baltic medians given as 201.5 to 207 days instead of “near 202”. References renumbered in order of first citation; plain explanations of Kaplan–Meier, the bootstrap and Spearman's correlation, and how the country median is computed, added.
